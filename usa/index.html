Most city guides are written from a desk somewhere else. They tell you the same twelve attractions in the same order, and they never mention that the museum is free on Thursday nights, that the famous bakery has a shorter line around the corner, or that the best view of the skyline is from the neighborhood across the water where nobody goes.

This is the index of every city we cover. Right now that means Boston, where we live, and where we have written guides to things to do, food and drink, and each of the neighborhoods worth walking, plus itineraries for one day, a weekend and three days. More US cities are being written now and will appear here when they are ready, not before.

Each guide follows the same format: an honest short answer at the top, ranked entries with addresses and the nearest transit stop, tables for anything with a schedule, and a FAQ for the questions people actually search. Start with a city below, or read how to use the guides if you want to know what to expect.

02

How to use these guides

If you are visiting for a few days, start with the city's itinerary guides. Boston has a 3-day itinerary, a weekend itinerary and a solo travel guide. These are built as day-by-day plans with walking order, so you can follow them as written or pull out the pieces you like.

If you live there and want a better weekend, go to the things-to-do hub and pick by mood: free, at night, on a rainy day, on a date, with friends. The seasonal guides (winter, summer, fall, spring) are updated as events are announced, and the "this weekend" page is refreshed every Thursday.

If you are hungry, the food and drink hub is organized by dish and by occasion rather than by star rating. Best lobster rolls, best cafés to work from, best bars to meet people, best cheap eats. Every entry has an address, the nearest transit stop, and a note on whether you need a reservation.

If you are deciding where to stay or move, the neighborhoods hub has a comparison table and a page per neighborhood covering what it is known for, what it costs, what the commute is like and where to eat and drink there.

If you only have an hour, the "this weekend" page and the free things to do page are the two fastest routes to a good afternoon. Both are organized so the entries closest to Downtown come first, and every entry says how long it takes.

Every page has a table of contents on the left and a "short answer" box at the top with the top eight to ten picks. If you read nothing else, read that box. Under each entry you will find a facts line with the address, the nearest transit stop, hours where they matter and what it costs, so you can decide without opening another tab.

04

How the guides are written

Every entry is a real place we have been to, with a real address and the correct nearest transit stop. Where we list hours or prices, they were accurate on the update date at the top of the page; where we are not confident, we say "check before you go" rather than guess. We do not include a place because it is famous, and we do not leave one out because it is.

Nothing in the guides is sponsored. Restaurants, bars and museums cannot pay to be listed or to move up a list. When a place charges admission, a cover, or is cash-only, we say so in the facts line under its name.

We try to write the way a local would answer if you asked them over coffee: here is the best one, here is why, here is the thing to know before you go. If we get something wrong, the byline on every page tells you who wrote it and when.

FAQ

City guides: common questions

Which cities do you cover?

Boston is the first city, with guides to things to do, food and drink, neighborhoods and multi-day itineraries. More US cities are in progress and will be added here as they are ready.

Who writes these guides?

People who live in the city. Every guide is written and checked by locals, not compiled from other travel sites, and each entry includes an address and the nearest transit stop so you can actually get there.

How often are the guides updated?

Each page shows its last update date under the title. Hours, prices and event schedules change often, so we revisit the popular pages regularly and note the date at the top. When we are not sure of a detail, we say check before you go.

Are the guides only for tourists?

No. About half of them are written for people who live in the city and want a better weekend, including guides to moving to Boston, making friends, and finding things to do alone or on a budget. The rest are for first-time visitors and weekend trips.

Do you accept paid placements in the guides?

No. Nothing in the guides is paid for, and we only include places we would send a friend to. Where a place charges admission or a cover, we say so.
