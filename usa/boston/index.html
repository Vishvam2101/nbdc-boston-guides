03

How to explore Boston

  • By neighborhood. Day one: Beacon Hill, Downtown, North End. Day two: Back Bay, museums, Esplanade. See the neighborhoods hub.
  • By interest. History: the Freedom Trail. Art: MFA, Gardner, ICA. Food: the food hub. Outdoors: Esplanade, Harborwalk.
  • By time. A few hours: Common to North End. Longer: the weekend or 3-day itinerary.

05

Getting around Boston

  • Walk first. Downtown to the North End is 10 minutes, Copley Square 20.
  • The T is $2.40 a ride, $22.50 for 7 days. Trains stop around 12:30am.
  • Red Line to Harvard, Green to Back Bay and museums, Blue to the airport, Orange to the South End.
  • The Silver Line SL1 is free from the airport.
  • The Long Wharf to Charlestown ferry is the cheapest harbor view in town.
  • Skip the car. Parking Downtown is $30 to $50 a day.

FAQ

Exploring Boston: common questions

How many days do you need to explore Boston?

Two full days covers the Freedom Trail, North End, Beacon Hill, Back Bay and one museum. Three days adds Cambridge and the harbor.

What is the best way to get around Boston?

Walking, then the T. Central neighborhoods are 30 to 40 minutes apart on foot and the subway is $2.40. You do not need a car.

When is the best time to visit Boston?

Late September through October for foliage, or May and June before the humidity. Winter is cold but hotels are cheaper.

Which Boston neighborhood should a first-time visitor stay in?

Back Bay or Downtown: walkable to most sights, most T lines. Beacon Hill is quieter, the Seaport has harbor views.

Is Boston expensive to visit?

Hotels are the expensive part, especially in fall and May. Sightseeing is cheap: the Freedom Trail, parks and several museum nights are free.
