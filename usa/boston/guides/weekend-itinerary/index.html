Boston · Itinerary guide

A weekend in Boston.
Friday night to Sunday afternoon.

The Boston weekend itinerary we give friends: where to eat Friday night, how to do the Freedom Trail and Back Bay in one Saturday, and what to do Sunday before the train, with seasonal versions and a budget.

By no bad days club, BostonUpdated September 8, 202613 min read

Boston is the best weekend city on the East Coast for a simple reason: you can get off a train at South Station on Friday at 6pm and be eating in the North End by 7:30 without seeing the inside of a car. The whole historic center fits inside a two-mile walk, the T runs past midnight, and the neighborhoods you most want to see, the North End, Downtown, Beacon Hill, Back Bay and the waterfront, are all next to each other. Cambridge is one stop across the river.

This weekend in Boston itinerary runs from Friday evening to Sunday afternoon and assumes you arrive by train or plane with no car. Friday night is dinner on Hanover Street and a walk along the harbor. Saturday is the big day: the Freedom Trail in the morning, Back Bay and Beacon Hill in the afternoon, sunset on the Esplanade, and a night out. Sunday is brunch, a choice between Cambridge and the Seaport, the SoWa market if it is running, and a train home. Each day has an hour-by-hour table, then the key stops with addresses and T stops.

If you have a third full day, use our Boston itinerary for 3 days instead; it spreads the same city out with room for a museum and a day trip. This page is the tighter version.

01

Friday evening: North End dinner and the Harborwalk

Friday is short and should be easy. Drop your bags, take the T or walk to Haymarket, eat well, and end on the waterfront. If you land late, cut the walk and keep the dinner; the North End serves until 10pm or later on Fridays, and the pastry shops are open until midnight.

Dinner in the North End

Giacomo's, 355 Hanover St · Neptune Oyster, 63 Salem St · Mamma Maria, 3 North SqHaymarket (Green/Orange)Most kitchens open until 10pm or later on Fridays$30–80 per person

The North End has around a hundred restaurants in a third of a square mile, and on a Friday night all of them are busy. Three that are worth it: Giacomo's, cash-only and no reservations, where the line is part of the experience and the seafood fra diavolo is the order; Neptune Oyster, tiny and excellent, where a solo diner or a couple gets seated far faster than a group; and Mamma Maria in the townhouse on North Square, the one to book a week ahead. Galleria Umberto and Regina Pizzeria are the cheap, great alternatives. Our North End guide ranks more of them.

Tip: Modern Pastry (257 Hanover) and Mike's Pastry (300 Hanover) are across the street from each other and Bostonians argue about them for sport. Modern fills to order, which is the correct answer. Get one of each and decide for yourself.

The Harborwalk after dark

Christopher Columbus Park, 110 Atlantic Ave, to Rowes WharfAquarium (Blue)Always open, well litFree

From the bottom of Hanover Street, cross Atlantic Avenue and you are in Christopher Columbus Park, under the long rose trellis that is lit at night. The Harborwalk runs south from here along Long Wharf, past the Aquarium, and through the great arch at Rowes Wharf. It is about a mile round trip, always busy enough to feel safe on a Friday, and the skyline over the water is the first view of Boston you will remember. In summer, the harbor cruise boats are still coming in and the Long Wharf bars are open.

02

Saturday: Freedom Trail, Back Bay, Beacon Hill, Esplanade sunset, night out

Saturday is the whole city in one day. It is roughly seven miles on foot with one T ride in the middle, and it works because Boston's historic core, its prettiest neighborhoods and its best sunset are all within a mile and a half of each other. Start at 9am; the Freedom Trail is calm until 10:30 and crowded after.

The Freedom Trail, Common to Old North Church

Starts at Boston Common Visitor Center, 139 Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)About 2 miles to the Old North Church, 2.5 hours with stopsFree to walk

Follow the red brick line in the sidewalk. On a weekend you want the first eleven sites, from the State House to the Old North Church, and you can leave the Charlestown half (USS Constitution and Bunker Hill) for another trip or a third day. The stops worth going inside: the Granary Burying Ground, where Paul Revere, Sam Adams and John Hancock are buried; the Old State House, with the Boston Massacre site in the road outside; and the Old North Church, where the lanterns were hung. The full Freedom Trail guide has every stop and what to skip.

The Public Garden, Newbury Street and the Boston Public Library

Public Garden, 4 Charles St · BPL, 700 Boylston StArlington or Copley (Green)Library Sat 9–5; Swan Boats mid-April to early SeptemberFree; Swan Boats about $4.50

The Green Line from Haymarket drops you at Arlington on the corner of the Public Garden, America's first public botanical garden, with the Swan Boats on the lagoon and the Make Way for Ducklings statues by the Beacon Street gate. Newbury Street starts at the Garden's west side and runs eight blocks of brownstone shopfronts; the blocks past Exeter are the interesting ones. Turn left on Exeter for the library. Bates Hall on the second floor is the best room in the city and it is open to anyone; the courtyard downstairs is where to sit. Trinity Church and the Hancock Tower are across Copley Square. See Back Bay for the full neighborhood.

Beacon Hill

Acorn St, between West Cedar and Willow · Louisburg Square · Charles StCharles/MGH (Red) or Park StreetAlways openFree

Walk back east through the Garden and cross Charles Street. Beacon Hill is a grid of brick sidewalks, gas lamps, and Federal townhouses that has barely changed since the 1840s. Acorn Street is the cobbled lane everyone photographs; Louisburg Square is the private park where the grandest houses are; Mount Vernon Street is the prettiest walk between them. Charles Street at the bottom has the shops, cafés and a couple of good bars. Our Beacon Hill guide has the route.

Sunset on the Esplanade

Hatch Memorial Shell, 47 David G. Mugar WayCharles/MGH (Red)Always openFree

From Charles Street, the Arthur Fiedler footbridge crosses Storrow Drive onto the Esplanade, the park along the Charles. Walk west to the dock in front of the Hatch Shell, which faces straight up the river into the sunset, with the MIT dome across the water and the Longfellow Bridge behind you. Sailboats from Community Boating are out until dusk from April to October. This is the place locals bring visitors on their first night, and the best of the sunset spots in Boston.

Saturday night out

Lookout Rooftop, 70 Sleeper St · Wally's Café, 427 Mass Ave · Laugh Boston, 425 Summer St · The Beehive, 541 Tremont StCourthouse (Silver) · Massachusetts Ave (Orange) · South Station · Back Bay (Orange)Most bars close 1–2amCocktails $15–18; shows $20–40

Boston's Saturday night is less of a scene than New York's and better for it. The Lookout at the Envoy in the Seaport is the rooftop with the skyline; the Beehive in the South End has live music and a late kitchen; Wally's has been a jazz club since 1947 and rarely charges cover; Laugh Boston in the Seaport books touring comics. If the Red Sox are home, an evening game at Fenway (4 Jersey St, Kenmore on the Green Line) replaces all of it. For more, see things to do in Boston at night and the best rooftop bars. The T runs until about 12:30am; after that, walk or take a taxi.

03

Sunday: brunch, Cambridge or the Seaport, SoWa and the train home

Sunday is a half-day with a choice in the middle. Cambridge is the classic: Harvard Yard, the free Harvard Art Museums and MIT's campus, all on the Red Line and back at South Station in fifteen minutes. The Seaport and the ICA are the alternative if the weather is bad or your train leaves from South Station and you want to stay close. From May to October, the SoWa Open Market in the South End is the best last stop before you go.

Brunch

The Friendly Toast, 35 Stanhope St · Mike & Patty's, 12 Church St · Lincoln Tavern, 425 W BroadwayBack Bay (Orange) · Arlington (Green) · Broadway (Red)Sunday from about 8am; lines by 10:30$15–30

Boston takes Sunday brunch seriously, which means lines. Go before 10am or after 1pm. The Friendly Toast in Back Bay is the big, loud, reliable one near the Orange Line. Mike & Patty's in Bay Village is a tiny counter with the best breakfast sandwich in the city and nowhere to sit, which is fine if you take it to the Public Garden. Lincoln Tavern in South Boston is worth the Red Line ride if you want a long, boozy brunch. The full ranking is in the best brunch in Boston.

Option A: Harvard Square and MIT

Harvard Yard, Massachusetts Ave · Harvard Art Museums, 32 Quincy St · MIT, 77 Massachusetts AveHarvard (Red) · Kendall/MIT (Red)Museums Sun 10–5, check before you goFree

Harvard is twelve minutes from Park Street on the Red Line. Walk into the Yard through Johnston Gate, past the statue and Widener Library, then out the far side to the Harvard Art Museums, free since 2023 and one of the best collections in the country under a Renzo Piano roof. Harvard Square's bookstores and cafés are good for an hour on their own. Then two stops back toward Boston to Kendall/MIT: the Stata Center, the Great Dome, and Killian Court, which has the best straight-on skyline view in Cambridge. Walk the Longfellow Bridge back into Boston; it is fifteen minutes and lands you at Charles/MGH. See Harvard Square.

Option B: the Seaport and the ICA

Institute of Contemporary Art, 25 Harbor Shore Dr · Row 34, 383 Congress StCourthouse (Silver Line SL1/SL2) or South Station (Red)ICA Sun 10–5, check before you goAbout $20

If your train leaves from South Station, the Seaport is a ten-minute walk away and keeps Sunday simple. The ICA is a small, sharp contemporary museum cantilevered over the harbor; the fourth-floor gallery with the glass wall facing the water is the point. Walk the Harborwalk along Fan Pier afterward for the skyline from the other side. Fort Point, across the Fort Point Channel, is the older brick half of the neighborhood and has the better lunch: oysters at Row 34 or a sandwich at Flour. Our Seaport guide covers both sides.

SoWa Open Market

460 Harrison Ave, South EndTufts Medical Center (Orange), 12-minute walk, or SL4/SL5 busSundays 10am–4pm, May through OctoberFree entry

SoWa ("south of Washington") is the South End's warehouse and gallery district, and on Sundays from May to October it hosts the biggest open-air market in the city: 150 or so makers and artists, a farmers' market, a row of food trucks and a covered vintage market inside the old power station. The galleries at 450 Harrison are open the same afternoon. It is a fifteen-minute walk to Back Bay Station and twenty to South Station, which makes it the ideal final stop. Outside market season, spend the time in the South End's cafés on Tremont Street instead.

04

Seasonal versions: foliage, winter, Marathon and summer weekends

A fall foliage weekend (late September to late October)

Boston's peak color is usually the third and fourth weeks of October. Keep the itinerary and swap the Sunday choice for trees: the Arnold Arboretum in Jamaica Plain (125 Arborway, Forest Hills on the Orange Line) is 281 acres of Harvard's tree collection and is free; Mount Auburn Cemetery in Cambridge (580 Mount Auburn St, bus 71 or 73 from Harvard) is the most beautiful landscape in the metro area and has a tower with a view to the city. The Commonwealth Avenue Mall and the Public Garden do the job downtown. The Head of the Charles Regatta runs the third weekend of October along the river from the Esplanade to Harvard; watch from the Weeks Footbridge in Cambridge. Book early that weekend. See things to do in Boston in the fall.

A winter weekend (December to February)

Cold, but the city is at its most beautiful in December. Keep Friday and Saturday as written, start the Freedom Trail at 10am for the sun, and take the ferry or the T rather than the bridge to Charlestown if you add it. Skate on the Frog Pond on the Common (rentals about $15, check before you go), see the trees on the Common and Copley Square and the lights on Commonwealth Avenue, and replace the Esplanade sunset with a 4pm walk on Beacon Hill, when the gas lamps come on. Swap Fenway for the Bruins or Celtics at TD Garden (100 Legends Way, North Station), ten minutes from the North End. SoWa is closed; the MFA or the Gardner takes its place on Sunday. Boston in winter has more.

Marathon weekend (Patriots' Day, the third Monday in April)

The Boston Marathon is a Monday, so the weekend before it is the busiest of the year. Hotels book out months ahead and rates double. If you are coming for it: the expo at the Hynes Convention Center runs Friday to Sunday, the B.A.A. 5K is Saturday morning around the Common, and Sunday is the day to walk Boylston Street while the finish line is being painted. On Monday, the Red Sox play at 11am at Fenway and the runners pass Kenmore Square in the early afternoon; the best places to watch are Coolidge Corner, Kenmore and the Boylston Street finish. Add Monday to this itinerary and move Sunday's Cambridge plan to Saturday afternoon.

A summer weekend (June to August)

Hot and humid, but the city moves outdoors. Add a harbor cruise or the ferry to Spectacle Island from Long Wharf on Sunday morning instead of Cambridge; it is twenty minutes out and has a swimming beach. Free concerts at the Hatch Shell run on summer evenings, Castle Island in South Boston has Sullivan's for fried clams and a walk around the fort, and the Lawn on D in the Seaport is open late. Memorial Day weekend is Boston Calling, the city's big music festival at the Harvard Athletic Complex. Fenway is in season, and evening games are the best thing to do with a summer Saturday night. See Boston in summer.

05

Where to stay for a weekend in Boston

For a weekend, stay somewhere you can walk to Friday's dinner and start Saturday on foot. Downtown and the waterfront are best for that; Back Bay is best for Saturday afternoon and Sunday morning. Weekend rates Downtown often drop because business travelers leave, which is worth knowing. Prices are typical ranges; check before you book.

Downtown and the waterfront

The Bostonian, 26 North St · Omni Parker House, 60 School St · Boston Harbor Hotel, 70 Rowes WharfHaymarket · Park Street · AquariumRoughly $230–700 a night; weekend rates often lower

The Bostonian faces Faneuil Hall and is a three-minute walk from Hanover Street, which makes Friday night effortless. The Parker House is on the Freedom Trail itself. The Boston Harbor Hotel is the splurge, on the water at Rowes Wharf. The financial district hotels a few blocks south (the Langham at 250 Franklin St, the Hyatt Centric at 54 Devonshire St) are often the best weekend deals in the city precisely because nobody is there on Saturdays.

Back Bay

The Lenox, 61 Exeter St · Copley Square Hotel, 47 Huntington Ave · The Newbury, 1 Newbury StCopley or Arlington (Green)Roughly $250–800 a night

Back Bay is the nicest place to wake up in Boston, and it is where Saturday afternoon and Sunday brunch happen. The Green Line runs to Haymarket for the North End in ten minutes, and Back Bay Station is the Amtrak stop, so arriving by train from New York puts you at the hotel in five minutes. The Lenox is the reliable classic; the Newbury on the corner of the Public Garden is the grand one. Where to stay in Boston has every neighborhood compared.

Budget and Cambridge

HI Boston, 19 Stuart St · citizenM North Station, 70 Causeway St · Kimpton Marlowe, 25 Edwin H. Land Blvd, CambridgeBoylston · North Station · Lechmere (Green)Hostel dorms roughly $50–90; citizenM $180–300; Marlowe $220–400

HI Boston is a clean, modern hostel a block from the Common with private rooms as well as dorms. citizenM by North Station is compact and well designed, a fifteen-minute walk to the North End and next to TD Garden for a winter game. Cambridge hotels near Kendall and Lechmere are frequently cheaper than Boston on weekends and are two Red Line stops from Downtown.

07

Getting there and getting around

From New York, Amtrak is the right call: the Acela takes about three hours forty minutes to South Station or Back Bay Station, both of which are in the middle of this itinerary, and the Northeast Regional is an hour slower and much cheaper. From Logan Airport, the Silver Line SL1 bus to South Station is free from the airport and takes about twenty minutes, or the Blue Line from Airport station serves Downtown and the waterfront. A taxi to the center is $25 to $40. If you drive, park the car for the weekend at the hotel (typically $40 to $60 a night) and do not touch it again.

In the city, you will walk most of this and use the T for four or five rides: a single subway fare is $2.40 by CharlieCard or tapping a contactless card, a one-day pass is $11, and a seven-day pass is $22.50. The Green Line covers Back Bay, the Public Garden and Fenway; the Red Line covers Cambridge and South Station; the Orange and Green lines reach Haymarket for the North End; the Blue Line reaches the Aquarium. Trains run until about 12:30am. Bluebikes, the bike-share, is $2.95 per half-hour ride and useful along the Esplanade. Our getting around Boston guide has the details, and Boston without a car is the case for leaving it at home.

08

Booking and timing for a Boston weekend

Book three things ahead: the hotel (Boston weekend rates climb fast in fall and around graduations in May), a Friday dinner reservation somewhere in the North End that takes them (Mamma Maria, Carmelina's, Bricco), and Red Sox tickets if the team is home. Everything else can be decided on the day. Restaurant lines in the North End peak from 7pm to 9pm on Friday and Saturday; eating at 6pm or after 9pm halves the wait, and a party of two gets seated far faster than four.

The best weekends are late September through October and late May through June. Avoid the weekend after Labor Day, when a hundred thousand students move in, and be aware that Marathon weekend, Head of the Charles weekend and any Harvard or MIT commencement weekend will fill every hotel. Many North End restaurants and pastry shops are cash-only. The MFA and the Gardner are closed Tuesdays, which does not affect a weekend, but the ICA is closed Mondays and Tuesdays, which matters if you are extending. Tipping is 18 to 20 percent.

FAQ

A weekend in Boston: common questions

Is a weekend enough time to see Boston?

Yes, if you arrive Friday evening. Two and a half days covers the Freedom Trail, the North End, Back Bay and Beacon Hill, the Esplanade, one museum or Cambridge, and three good dinners. The city is compact enough that you never spend more than twenty minutes in transit.

What is the best weekend to visit Boston?

Late September through mid-October has the best weather, the Head of the Charles regatta and the start of foliage. Late May and June are the green, warm alternative. Marathon weekend in April is exciting but hotels sell out months ahead. Avoid the first weekend of college move-in at the start of September.

Where should I stay for a weekend in Boston?

Back Bay or Downtown. Both put Saturday on foot and are one short T ride from the North End and Cambridge. Weekend rates Downtown are often lower than weekdays because business travel drops off, so check the financial district hotels first.

How much does a weekend in Boston cost?

Excluding travel to the city, roughly $250 to $350 per person for a hostel weekend with cheap eats, $700 to $1,000 for a mid-range hotel with dinners out, and $1,500 or more for a top hotel, tasting menus and a Red Sox game. Transit is about $11 a day or $22.50 for a 7-day pass.

Do I need a car for a weekend in Boston?

No, and it will cost you $40 to $60 a night to park one. Everything in this itinerary is walkable or on the T, and Amtrak drops you at South Station or Back Bay Station, both in the middle of the plan.

What should I do in Boston on a Sunday?

Brunch, then either Harvard Square and MIT in Cambridge or the Seaport and the ICA. From May to October, the SoWa Open Market in the South End runs every Sunday from 10am to 4pm and is the best way to spend a final couple of hours before you leave.
