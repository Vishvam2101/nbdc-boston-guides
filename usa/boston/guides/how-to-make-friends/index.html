01

Why it's hard at first

Boston is transient: 35-plus colleges plus hospitals and biotech mean many people leave after two years, so aim for a wider circle than you think you need. It is reserved: nobody chats on the T, and friendliness is shown by showing up. And winter is long, so build a base by December.

02 · Sweat & outdoors

Run clubs, rec leagues and hobby communities

Free, weekly and built for strangers. Leagues put you on a team for two months.

November Project

Harvard Stadium, AllstonFree, Wed 6:30am

Free stadium-stair workouts with a "newbie" welcome every week. Show up three Wednesdays in a row and someone invites you to breakfast.

Local tip · Friday workouts are smaller and more conversational.

Heartbreak Hill Running Company runs

652 Tremont St, South EndFree

Free evening runs with split pace groups, nobody dropped, and a bar at the end. Skews 25 to 40 and heavy on recent arrivals.

Tracksmith Trackhouse runs

285 Newbury StFree

Group runs along the Charles for slightly more serious runners. Pick this for training partners rather than a party.

Slow AF Run Club and no-drop groups

Esplanade or Cambridge bankFree

JP, Somerville and Southie groups run at conversational pace and welcome walkers. The whole point is talking.

Social Boston Sports and Volo leagues

Fields & gyms citywide$60–100/season

Co-ed kickball, softball, volleyball, dodgeball. Sign up as a free agent and your whole team is also new; games end at a sponsor bar.

Local tip · Fall sign-ups open in August; free agent spots go first.

Community Boating on the Charles

21 David G. Mugar Way~$200–400/season

Unlimited sailing for a season plus free lessons taught by other members. The dock on a summer evening is a standing social scene.

Learn-to-row programs

Community Rowing, Brighton

A boat with seven strangers at 6am, moving in unison. Sessions run a few weeks and feed into recreational crews.

Boston Ski & Sports Club

Bus trips citywide

Ski bus trips to New Hampshire and Vermont, plus its own leagues. The winter answer to January through March.

Hiking and the outdoors

AMC, Blue Hills ReservationFree/low cost

The AMC Boston chapter runs beginner hikes most weekends, many car-free. A small-group hike is four hours of conversation.

03 · Social & games

Trivia, board games, climbing and online

Something to do with your hands and a reason to talk.

Weekly pub trivia, same bar every week

The Burren, SomervilleFree to play

Tell the host you're alone and they'll put you on a team. Go to the same bar every week and by week four it's your team.

Local tip · Trident's Tuesday trivia in Back Bay is especially newcomer-heavy.

Board game night at Knight Moves

1402 Beacon St, BrooklineCover for open play

A board game café with over a thousand games where staff match solo visitors to tables that need a player.

A climbing gym membership

Central Rock Gym, Cambridge~$90/month

Bouldering is 30 seconds of climbing and five minutes of talking about the route. Go twice a week and you have a crew within a month.

Local tip · Both gyms run intro classes that put you in a group of beginners day one.

Meetup groups

Citywide

Pick groups with a fixed weekly or monthly activity: a Blue Hills hike, a Thursday game night. A hike with 12 beats a bar with 60.

Local tip · Filter for groups that have met at least ten times.

Bumble BFF

AppFree

A real user base here, mostly women in their 20s and 30s who moved for work or grad school. Use it for an activity partner, not a friend.

Local tip · Suggest a specific recurring thing in the first three messages.

Coworking and work-from-home cafés

Workbar, CIC Cambridge

Workbar and CIC run member socials. Cheaper: work from one café the same two mornings a week; regulars notice regulars.

04 · Volunteer

Volunteering: the most reliable channel nobody uses

The same 10 people every week, working on something together, with nothing to buy.

Community Servings kitchen shifts

179 Amory St, JPFree

Chop vegetables for medically tailored meals alongside 15 to 20 people for three hours. Book a recurring slot.

Rosie's Place

889 Harrison Ave, South EndFree

A sanctuary for poor and homeless women. Lunch and dinner shifts with a regular volunteer pool that mixes ages and neighborhoods.

Cradles to Crayons

155 N Beacon St, BrightonFree

Sort donated clothes and school supplies in two-hour shifts with a room full of other people in their 20s. Easiest first try.

05 · Learn

Classes and language exchanges

Cambridge Center for Adult Education

42 Brattle St$50–300

Cooking, pottery, languages, woodworking, since 1938. Pick a multi-week hands-on course, not a one-night workshop.

Language exchanges

Harvard Sq, Kendall, Back BayFree or a drink

Weekly Spanish, French, Mandarin and Japanese nights, mostly via Meetup. You don't need to be fluent; half the room is also new.

Cooking classes and studio memberships

BCAE, Mudflat Studio

A pottery membership means seeing the same people at the wheel at odd hours. Cooking classes are easy to attend alone.

Local tip · Mudflat's classes have a long waitlist. Get on it in your first month.

06

Church, temple and mosque communities

Most large congregations run young-adult groups with weekly dinners and service projects. Trinity Church in Copley Square (Episcopal); the Paulist Center and Park Street Church at Park Street; Temple Israel and the Vilna Shul for young-adult Jewish programming; the Islamic Society of Boston Cultural Center in Roxbury Crossing, the largest mosque in New England. Not religious? Arlington Street Church and the Boston Ethical Society welcome the unsure.

Your first 90 days: a plan

A casual friend takes about 50 shared hours, a close one about 200. Weekly beats one-off.

Week 1Pick an anchor weeknight activity and go onceSomething on the calendar before the boxes are unpacked
Week 2Register as a free agent for the next rec league seasonLocks in 6 to 8 weeks of the same people
Weeks 2–4Go to the anchor every week. Say yes to the after-thingThe bar or breakfast after is where friends are made

FAQ

How to make friends in Boston: common questions

Is it hard to make friends in Boston?

At first, yes. The city is reserved, transient and indoors all winter. But it is small and full of weekly activities. Show up to the same thing weekly for three months and it gets easier.

What is the best way to meet people in Boston in your 20s?

Recurring activities where strangers are expected: November Project, Heartbreak Hill runs, Social Boston Sports and Volo leagues, weekly pub trivia, climbing gyms and volunteering shifts.

Which Boston neighborhoods are best for meeting people?

Allston, Brighton, Somerville (Davis and Union Square), Cambridge (Central and Inman), the South End and Southie. Most roommates, run clubs, trivia nights and casual bars, all walkable.

How long does it take to make friends after moving to Boston?

Most people find months one to three lonely and months four to six fine. Casual friendship takes about 50 shared hours, close friendship about 200, so weekly activities beat one-off events.

How do I make friends in Boston in the winter?

Keep it indoors and weekly: climbing gyms, board games at Knight Moves, trivia, adult ed classes, Boston Ski and Sports Club trips, indoor rec leagues and volunteering shifts.
