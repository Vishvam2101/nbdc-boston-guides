Boston is small enough that a real bucket list is achievable in a long weekend, and specific enough that a generic "top things to do" list undersells it. This is 40 items: the icons everyone should see once, the food that defines the city, the views worth the walk, the events that only happen on a certain date, a Cambridge half-day, day trips within reach, and the small local rituals that make a trip feel lived-in rather than toured.

Every item has a real address and the nearest T stop. Where something costs money or needs a season, we say so, rather than promising it is free or always available. Treat the checklist at the bottom as a printable version to work through at your own pace, not a race to finish in one visit.

This list assumes no particular interest or budget. If you want it pre-sorted into days, see our 3-day itinerary and weekend itinerary, which schedule a large chunk of this list hour by hour.

01

The icons: see these once

The things that show up on every postcard, and are worth doing anyway.

Walk the Freedom Trail

Starts at Boston Common, 139 Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)2.5 miles, 2–3 hours

Sixteen historic sites connected by a red brick line, from Boston Common to the USS Constitution. The single best orientation to the city. Full stop-by-stop route in our Freedom Trail guide.

See a game at Fenway Park

4 Jersey St, FenwayKenmore or Fenway (Green)Tickets from around $25; tours run on non-game days

The oldest ballpark in Major League Baseball, in use since 1912. Even a tour, walking the Green Monster seats, is worth it if there's no game on your dates.

Spend an afternoon at the Museum of Fine Arts

465 Huntington Ave, FenwayMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)~$25; pay what you wish Wed after 4pm

One of the largest art museums in the US. Pick a wing rather than trying to see everything: the Art of the Americas, the Japanese garden, or the Egyptian galleries.

See the Public Garden and the Make Way for Ducklings statues

4 Charles StArlington (Green)Free; Swan Boats a few dollars, April–September

America's oldest public botanical garden, next to Boston Common. The bronze duckling statues near the Beacon Street corner are a Boston rite of passage for photos.

Photograph Acorn Street

Acorn St, Beacon HillCharles/MGH (Red)Free, public street

A cobblestone lane often called the most photographed street in America. Go on a weekday evening to get it without a crowd of tripods.

Take a harbor cruise or the Charlestown ferry

Long Wharf, 1 Long WharfAquarium (Blue)Ferry costs a standard T fare; cruises from about $30

The cheapest way to see Boston from the water is the MBTA ferry to the Charlestown Navy Yard. For a longer trip, whale watching and sunset cruises leave from the same wharf in season.

02

Food to try

Eat a hot buttered lobster roll

Neptune Oyster, 63 Salem St · Row 34, 383 Congress StPrices move with the lobster market, expect $30–45

The New England style, warm with butter, is the one to try first. Full ranking in best lobster rolls in Boston.

Have a cannoli in the North End

Mike's Pastry, 300 Hanover St · Modern Pastry, 257 Hanover StNorth End

Boston's longest-running dessert rivalry. Details and the full neighborhood guide at things to do in the North End.

A bowl of clam chowder

Union Oyster House, 41 Union StGovernment Center (Blue/Green)

America's oldest continuously operating restaurant, open since 1826, still serves a classic New England chowder at its raw bar.

A slice from Regina Pizzeria

11½ Thacher St, North EndHaymarket (Orange/Green)

Thin, charred at the edges, from the original brick oven since 1926. Widely considered Boston's best pizza.

An oyster at a raw bar

Neptune Oyster, 63 Salem St · B&G Oysters, 550 Tremont StAround $3–4 per oyster

Massachusetts oysters, especially from the North Shore and Cape Cod, are some of the best on the East Coast.

03

Views to see

Watch sunset from the Charles River Esplanade

Access from Arlington St or the Fiedler FootbridgeCharles/MGH (Red)Free

Faces west toward Cambridge, so it gets the sunset every evening. The dock by the Hatch Shell is the spot.

The skyline from Piers Park, East Boston

95 Marginal St, East BostonMaverick (Blue), 10-minute walkFree

The best free view of the Downtown skyline, across the harbor and largely undiscovered by tourists.

View Boston, atop the Prudential Center

800 Boylston St, Back BayPrudential (Green E) or Copley (Green)Check current pricing and hours before you go

An indoor observation deck with 360-degree views over the whole city, useful on a day when walking outside isn't appealing.

05

Cambridge

Walk through Harvard Yard

Harvard University, CambridgeHarvard (Red)Free to walk; free student-led tours run most days

The historic core of the oldest university in the US. The Harvard Art Museums two blocks away are free every day.

See MIT's campus and the Charles from the Cambridge side

77 Massachusetts Ave, CambridgeKendall/MIT (Red)Free

The Great Dome and the Stata Center are worth a short detour, and the riverside path back to the Longfellow Bridge has the best angle on the Back Bay skyline.

Kayak or paddle the Charles River

Community Boating, 21 David G Mugar WayCharles/MGH (Red)Rentals from around $25/hour in season

Paddling between the Back Bay and Cambridge skylines is one of the more memorable ways to spend a summer afternoon in Boston.

06

Day trips

Salem

Commuter rail from North StationAbout 35 minutes each way

Witch trial history, a walkable harbor town, and the Peabody Essex Museum. Especially busy in October.

The Boston Harbor Islands

Ferry from Long WharfSeasonal, roughly May–October

A national park a short ferry ride from downtown, with beaches, hiking and a Civil War-era fort on Georges Island.

Cape Cod

About 1.5–2 hours by car or seasonal ferry

Best as an overnight or a long summer day trip. Provincetown and the Cape Cod National Seashore are the highlights.

Walden Pond, Concord

915 Walden St, ConcordCommuter rail to Concord, then a short walk or rideshare

Thoreau's pond, plus Concord's Revolutionary War sites, make an easy half-day out of the city.

Plymouth

About 45 minutes by car; seasonal ferry from Boston in summer

Plymouth Rock, the Mayflower II and a walkable historic waterfront.

07

Local rites

Buy fruit at Haymarket

Blackstone St, near Government CenterFridays and Saturdays

The oldest open-air produce market in the country: loud, cash-based and very cheap.

A hot dog at Sullivan's, Castle Island

2010 William J Day Blvd, South BostonBus 9 or 11 from Broadway (Red)

A takeout stand at a 19th-century harbor fort, selling cheap hot dogs and clam plates since 1951, with planes from Logan passing overhead.

A Bruins or Celtics game at TD Garden

100 Legends WayNorth Station (Orange/Green)Ticket prices vary widely by opponent and season

Two of the most storied franchises in their sports, in a downtown arena you can walk to.

Sunday at SoWa Open Market

460 Harrison Ave, South EndTufts Medical Center (Orange)Sundays 11–4, May–October

About 200 vendors, food trucks and a vintage market in a former power station.

FAQ

Boston bucket list: common questions

What are the must-do things in Boston?

Walk the Freedom Trail, see a game at Fenway Park, eat a lobster roll and a cannoli, watch the sunset from the Charles River Esplanade, and spend an afternoon in Cambridge at Harvard Yard. Those five cover the essentials most visitors regret skipping.

How many days do you need to do the Boston bucket list?

The core 20 items fit into 3 to 4 days. Adding a day trip to Salem or Cape Cod, or timing a trip around Head of the Charles or the Marathon, is easier with 5 to 7 days.

What is the number one thing to do in Boston?

For most visitors, walking the Freedom Trail from Boston Common to the North End captures the city best: history, architecture and food in one 2.5-mile walk.

Is the Boston bucket list different in winter?

Yes. Swan Boats, Harbor Islands ferries and outdoor markets close for the season, but the Freedom Trail, museums, Fenway tours, TD Garden games and the holiday lights on Commonwealth Avenue all still apply, and the Christmas tree lighting becomes a seasonal must.

What Boston foods should be on every bucket list?

A hot buttered lobster roll, a cannoli from the North End, clam chowder, a Regina pizza, and an oyster at a raw bar. Those five dishes cover the city's signature flavors without repeating a cuisine.
