Boston has a funny relationship with coffee. Dunkin' started in Quincy, ten minutes down the Red Line. George Howell started here too, and his Coffee Connection was one of the first places in America to sell single-origin beans. Somewhere between those two you get Hanover Street espresso bars that haven't changed since the 1930s and a crop of young roasters in Cambridge and Somerville.

We've sat in every one of these, some of them for whole afternoons on deadline. We'll tell you which ones are for staying and which are for a quick shot at the counter.

Most open early and close mid-afternoon. A cappuccino costs $4.50 to $6, a bit less in the North End.

01 · Downtown

Downtown & the Leather District

The serious coffee is here. Three of the best espresso bars in the city are ten minutes apart, right off the Common.

George Howell Coffee

505 Washington StDowntown CrossingLaptops: sometimes

A long marble bar run by the man who brought single-origin coffee to Boston. Staff will talk you through a pour-over for as long as you want. Order a filter from the reserve list. Open early to late afternoon.

Order off the "cup of excellence" list. A few dollars more, and the best cup in the city.

Gracenote Coffee

108 Lincoln StSouth StationNo laptops

A closet-sized espresso bar that a lot of Boston coffee people will tell you is the best in town. Light roasts, pulled with care. Order espresso or a cortado. Weekdays, mornings to mid-afternoon.

Ogawa Coffee

10 Milk StStateLaptops: yes

A Kyoto roaster's first cafe outside Japan. Blond wood, tiered seating over the bar and very good latte art. Order a latte. Weekdays from early; weekends vary.

Thinking Cup

165 Tremont StPark StreetLaptops: sometimes

A great cappuccino across from the Common. Narrow, dark wood, always full. Order a cappuccino and a pastry. Daily, early to evening.

02 · Back Bay & Beacon Hill

Back Bay & Beacon Hill

Bigger rooms, comfier chairs. Good for an hour off your feet after Newbury or Charles Street.

Tatte Bakery & Café

70 Charles StCharles/MGHLaptops: sometimes

Boston argues about Tatte. We think the food is good and the Charles Street room is the prettiest one they have. Order shakshuka or a pistachio croissant. Daily, early to early evening.

Weekend lines are long. Go on a weekday or before 9am.

Pavement Coffeehouse

1096 Boylston StHynes Convention CenterLaptops: yes

Where Boston goes to sit with a laptop for three hours. Long tables, plenty of outlets, very good bagels. Order an egg and cheese on a house bagel. Daily, early to evening.

Blue Bottle Coffee

163 Newbury StCopleyNo laptops

White walls, a short menu and no Wi-Fi on purpose. A calm basement halfway down Newbury. Order the New Orleans iced coffee. Daily, morning to late afternoon.

Blackbird Doughnuts

492 Tremont StBack BayNo laptops

A doughnut counter, not somewhere to sit. The brioche doughnuts are the best in Boston and the coffee holds up. Order the Bismarck. Morning until sold out.

Newbury Street, the Common and Beacon Hill are all walkable from any of these. Our Boston weekend itinerary builds a full day around this stretch of the city. See also the Beacon Hill guide and best brunch.

03 · South End

South End & Fort Point

Boston's best cafe neighborhood. Brownstone streets and three cafes that would top the list in most cities.

Render Coffee

563 Columbus AveMassachusetts AveLaptops: yes

A bright room, a back patio most people miss, and staff who remember your order. Order a breakfast sandwich and a guest espresso. Daily, early to late afternoon.

Walk in from Copley instead of Mass Ave. The streets are much prettier.

Flour Bakery + Café

1595 Washington StMassachusetts AveLaptops: sometimes

Joanne Chang's sticky bun is famous, and it's as good as people say. The Fort Point branch is handy for the Seaport. Order the sticky bun, warm. Daily, early to early evening.

Jaho Coffee & Wine Bar

1651 Washington StMassachusetts AveLaptops: yes

The biggest cafe in the South End and one of the few open late. Coffee in the day, wine at night. Order nitro cold brew, then wine. Daily, morning to late evening.

Cafe-hop your way through the South End and it starts to feel like a scavenger hunt. Our Boston city game turns an afternoon like that into an actual challenge. See also the Seaport guide and best brunch.

04 · North End

The North End

Old-school Italian espresso bars, some open since before the Depression. Go for the room as much as the coffee.

Caffè Vittoria

290 Hanover StHaymarketNo laptops · Cash preferred

Open since 1929, with tin ceilings and antique espresso machines. The best dessert stop after dinner on Hanover. Order a cappuccino and cannoli. Daily, morning to late night.

Polcari's Coffee

105 Salem StHaymarketNo laptops · Cash preferred

A coffee and spice shop on the same corner since 1932. Sacks of beans, jars of spices. Very few shops like it are left. Order a pound of house espresso. Mon–Sat, daytime.

The North End is usually the first stop on any Boston trip, coffee included. See where it fits in our 3-day Boston itinerary. See also the North End guide and cheap eats.

05 · Cambridge

Cambridge

More cafes per block than anywhere in Greater Boston, most of them full of grad students. These three are worth crossing the river for.

Broadsheet Coffee Roasters

100 Kirkland StHarvardLaptops: yes

Our favorite all-round coffee shop in Cambridge. A big, bright roastery with long tables, and nobody minds laptops. Order espresso and the breakfast sandwich. Daily, early to afternoon.

1369 Coffee House

757 Massachusetts AveCentralLaptops: yes

Central Square's coffee house since 1993, with mismatched chairs and a bulletin board buried in flyers. It hasn't really changed. Order iced coffee and a scone. Daily, early to evening.

Curio Coffee

441 Cambridge StLechmereNo laptops

Tiny. Rotating coffee from small roasters and Belgian waffles made to order. Order a Liège waffle and a cortado. Mornings to early afternoon.

Long tables and Wi-Fi that doesn't quit make Cambridge's cafes some of the best solo work spots in the city. More on that in our Boston solo travel guide. See also the Harvard Square guide.

06 · Somerville

Somerville: Davis & Porter

Up the Red Line, where cafes stay open after dark and nobody minds if you stay all afternoon.

Diesel Café

257 Elm StDavisLaptops: yes

The living room of Davis Square since 1999, pool tables in the back. The easiest place in Greater Boston to open a laptop. Order a latte and a game of pool. Daily, early to evening.

Open into the evening, which almost nothing else here is.

Forge Baking Company

626 Somerville AvePorterLaptops: yes

The bakery-roastery behind Diesel. A strong pastry case, and an ice cream bar next door for the evening. Order a morning bun. Daily, early to evening.

Diesel and Forge both make it onto our Boston bucket list of things every local ends up doing at least once. See also bars to meet people.

FAQ

Boston cafes: common questions

What is the best cafe in Boston?

For coffee alone, George Howell at the Godfrey Hotel downtown. For food, room and all of it together, Tatte on Charles Street in Beacon Hill.

Which Boston cafes are best for working on a laptop?

Diesel in Davis Square, Broadsheet in Cambridge, Render in the South End, Pavement on Boylston and 1369 in Central Square. Skip Blue Bottle, Gracenote and Vittoria for this.

Where is the best espresso in Boston?

Gracenote on Lincoln Street, then George Howell. For a classic Italian shot, Caffè Vittoria on Hanover.

Does the North End have good coffee shops?

Yes, in the Italian style: espresso, cappuccino and pastry. For pour-over, walk to George Howell downtown.

Which cafes stay open latest?

Most close between 4 and 6pm. Jaho, Vittoria, Diesel and Forge stay open into the evening. Hours change with the seasons, so check first.

Are Boston cafes cash only?

Nearly all take cards. Vittoria and Polcari's in the North End still prefer cash.
