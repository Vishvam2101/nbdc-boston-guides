Brunch in Boston has a few local quirks worth knowing before you set out. There is no bottomless mimosa culture here, because state law does not allow it. The best-loved spots are mostly small, mostly walk-in only, and mostly full by 10am on a Sunday. And a surprising number of the city's best brunches are not at brunch restaurants at all but at bakery cafes, where you order at the counter, pay $14, and eat better than at most white-tablecloth Sunday services.

This guide lists 15 brunch spots across Boston, Brookline and Cambridge, sorted by neighborhood so you can pick the one nearest to where you wake up. Each entry has the address, the nearest T stop, a price range and what to order. After the neighborhood lists, there are sections on the best brunch for groups, the cheapest good brunch, what "boozy brunch" realistically means in Massachusetts, weekday options, and a practical section on beating the wait.

Hours and prices here are as of September 2026. Brunch hours in particular shift with the season and the staffing, so check the restaurant's site before a long trip across town.

01

Brunch in Beacon Hill and Downtown

Charles Street is the classic Boston brunch block: a diner, a bakery cafe and gas lamps, all within a hundred yards. Bay Village, tucked behind the Theater District, has the city's best breakfast sandwich.

Paramount

44 Charles St, Beacon HillCharles/MGH (Red)Breakfast daily from early morning; weekend line$12–22 per person

The Paramount has been feeding Beacon Hill since 1937, and the weekend routine has not changed much: you queue on Charles Street, order cafeteria-style at the counter, wait for your plate, and only then take a seat. It sounds chaotic and it works. The banana caramel French toast is the dish people cross the city for; the omelets and corned beef hash are the dishes locals actually order every week. Portions are big and the price is fair for the neighborhood.

Local tip: the rule is no seat until you have food in hand. Do not send a friend to grab a table, it will not go well. Come before 9am on weekends or after 1pm.

Tatte Bakery & Café

70 Charles St, Beacon Hill, plus many locationsCharles/MGH (Red)Opens early daily; brunch menu all day$12–22 per person

Tatte started as a single pastry stand and is now the most visible cafe brand in Boston, with locations in nearly every neighborhood. The Beacon Hill original on Charles Street is the prettiest. The shakshuka, the halloumi and egg sandwiches and the pastry case (the pistachio croissant, the nut tarts) are consistently good, and the counter-service model means you rarely wait more than ten minutes for a table even on Sunday. It is not the most exciting brunch on this list, but it is the most reliable, and it is open early.

Mike & Patty's

12 Church St, Bay VillageArlington (Green)Mornings into early afternoon; check hours$9–15 per person

Mike & Patty's is a corner shop the size of a large closet, with a handful of seats and a line of people waiting for breakfast sandwiches that are among the best in the country. The Fancy (egg, bacon, cheddar, avocado, red onion and house mayo on multigrain) is the one to order first. There is nowhere to linger, so this is brunch for people who want to eat well and then walk to the Public Garden, which is two blocks away.

02

Brunch in Back Bay and the South End

Back Bay has the biggest rooms and the most reservation-friendly brunches. The South End, a ten-minute walk south, has the neighborhood-restaurant version.

Buttermilk & Bourbon

160 Commonwealth Ave, Back BayCopley or Arlington (Green)Weekend brunch; reservations recommended$25–40 per person

Jason Santos's New Orleans-inspired restaurant does the most fun brunch in Back Bay: warm honey-glazed biscuits, chicken and waffles, shrimp and grits, and a big, loud, purple-lit room that feels like a party by noon. It is the place for a birthday brunch or an out-of-town guest who wants a scene. It is also one of the few on this list where a reservation for eight on a Sunday is actually possible.

Trident Booksellers & Café

338 Newbury St, Back BayHynes Convention Center (Green)Open daily, breakfast served all day$14–22 per person

Trident is an independent bookstore with a full cafe, and it has served breakfast all day since long before that was fashionable. The menu is enormous: pancakes, benedicts, breakfast burritos, huevos rancheros, a good veggie hash. The upstairs dining room fills up on weekends, but because it is open from morning until late, you can have "brunch" at 4pm on a Wednesday and browse the shelves afterward. It is the best rainy-day brunch in the city.

The Friendly Toast

35 Stanhope St, Back Bay (also 1 Kendall Sq, Cambridge)Back Bay (Orange) or Kendall/MIT (Red)Daily, breakfast all day$15–25 per person

The Friendly Toast is a small New England chain with a kitschy, thrift-store dining room and a menu built for hangovers: enormous pancakes, stuffed French toast, breakfast burritos the size of a forearm, and a long list of Bloody Marys. It is not subtle, and it is not meant to be. Both locations are big enough to absorb a group, and the Kendall Square one is the easiest brunch on the Cambridge side to get into without a wait.

Cafe Landwer

383 Boylston St, Back Bay (also 900 Beacon St, Fenway)Arlington (Green) or St. Mary's Street (Green C)Daily, breakfast all day$15–25 per person

Landwer is an Israeli cafe chain, and its Boston branches serve the Tel Aviv version of brunch: shakshuka in several variations, an "Israeli breakfast" platter with eggs, salads, cheeses and bread, labneh, halloumi, and very good coffee. Both locations are bright and roomy, take reservations, and serve the whole menu all day, which makes Landwer a strong option for a late or weekday brunch.

Stephi's on Tremont

571 Tremont St, South EndBack Bay (Orange), 10-minute walkWeekend brunch; reservations available$20–35 per person

Stephi's is the South End's neighborhood brunch: a comfortable, slightly grown-up dining room on Tremont Street with lobster benedict, brioche French toast, a good burger and a proper cocktail list. It is what you want when you would like to sit for two hours with a Bloody Mary and not be shouted at. The sidewalk tables are the best seats when the weather cooperates, and the walk afterward along Tremont and through Union Park is one of the South End's pleasures.

03

Brunch in the North End and Fenway

North Street Grille

229 North St, North EndHaymarket (Orange/Green)Breakfast and brunch daily; check hours$15–28 per person

The North End is famous for dinner and cannoli, not breakfast, which is why North Street Grille matters: it is the neighborhood's one serious brunch spot. The menu is big American brunch with an Italian accent, and the portions are large. The lobster benedict and the stuffed French toast are the regulars' orders. It is a few doors from the Paul Revere House, so it works well as the first stop on a Freedom Trail day, before the crowds.

Local tip: get the brunch here, then get your cannoli on Hanover Street, not the other way around. Our North End guide settles the cannoli question.

Flour Bakery + Café

12 Farnsworth St, Fort Point, plus locations in Back Bay, South End, Fenway, Cambridge and moreSouth Station (Red/Silver) for Fort PointOpens early daily$8–16 per person

Joanne Chang's Flour is the bakery that every other Boston bakery gets compared to, and any of its locations makes an excellent, fast, inexpensive brunch. The sticky bun is the famous item and it deserves to be. The egg sandwich on focaccia, the breakfast pizza when it appears, the quiche and the granola with yogurt are all reliable. It is counter service, seating is limited and turns fast, and a full breakfast with coffee lands around $15.

Cafe Landwer's Beacon Street location, listed above, covers the Fenway side. For ballgame-day breakfast see our Fenway guide.

04

Brunch in Cambridge, Brookline and Allston

Across the river and down the Green Line are some of the best brunches in the area, and generally shorter waits than in central Boston.

Sofra Bakery & Café

1 Belmont St, Cambridge (Huron Village)Harvard (Red), then bus 72 or 73, or a 25-minute walkMornings and afternoons; check hours$10–20 per person

Sofra is Ana Sortun's Turkish and Middle Eastern bakery on the Cambridge and Watertown line, and it is the best breakfast in Greater Boston that most visitors never find. Shakshuka, Turkish breakfast plates with sesame bread and haloumi, a morning bun with orange blossom, savory pastries and Turkish coffee. It is counter service with a handful of seats inside and a few benches out front, and it gets busy on weekend mornings. Worth the bus ride from Harvard Square.

Henrietta's Table

1 Bennett St, Charles Hotel, Harvard SquareHarvard (Red)Sunday brunch buffet; reservations strongly recommendedRoughly $55–70 per person; check current price

Henrietta's Table is the farm-to-table restaurant in the Charles Hotel, and its Sunday brunch is a proper New England buffet: local eggs, smoked fish, a carving station, pancakes, a large spread of baked goods and dessert. It is the most expensive brunch on this list and the most old-fashioned, in a good way. It is the place for a graduation, a parents' visit or a big family table. Book ahead; Harvard weekends fill it a month out.

Loyal Nine

660 Cambridge St, East CambridgeLechmere (Green)Weekend brunch; confirm they are open and check hours before you go$18–30 per person

Loyal Nine cooks New England food with a historian's interest in it, and brunch there means johnnycakes, smoked fish, house-baked breads and eggs done with more care than the neighborhood expects. East Cambridge is quiet on weekend mornings and the room is calm. The restaurant's hours and format have shifted more than once in recent years, so confirm they are serving brunch before you make the trip.

Zaftigs Delicatessen

335 Harvard St, Brookline (Coolidge Corner)Coolidge Corner (Green C)Breakfast all day, daily$15–25 per person

Zaftigs is a Jewish deli in Coolidge Corner that serves breakfast all day, every day, and on weekends the line down Harvard Street is a Brookline institution. The banana-stuffed French toast is the signature; the potato latkes, the challah French toast and the corned beef hash are the sensible orders. Portions are huge. It is the best brunch on the Green Line C branch and a good excuse to explore Coolidge Corner's bookstore and cinema afterward.

Lulu's Allston

421 Cambridge St, AllstonHarvard Ave (Green B), 10-minute walk, or bus 66Weekend brunch; reservations available$15–25 per person

Lulu's is the Allston brunch: a big, casual room, a menu of chicken and waffles, breakfast tacos, fried chicken sandwiches and a long cocktail list, and a crowd that is mostly students and people who were students recently. Prices are lower than the Back Bay equivalents. It is also one of the few places in the neighborhood that takes brunch reservations, which matters on a Sunday when the rest of Allston is asleep until noon.

05

Best brunch in Boston for groups

Boston's most beloved brunch spots are small, and a table for eight at Paramount or Mike & Patty's is not a thing that exists. For a group, you want a big room and a reservation. The four that deliver that most reliably:

Henrietta's Table is the first choice for a family or a mixed-age group, because a buffet solves every dietary argument and the Charles Hotel handles large parties every weekend. Buttermilk & Bourbon is the choice for a birthday or bachelorette-style brunch where the point is noise and cocktails. The Friendly Toast, at either location, has the square footage for ten people and a menu that will please the pickiest eater in the group. Cafe Landwer on Boylston is the calm option: roomy, reservable, and open all day so you can book a 1pm table when the morning rush has cleared.

If your group is up for it, the alternative is to skip the reservation and do bakery brunch: send two people ahead to grab tables at a Tatte or Flour, and let everyone order at the counter. It is cheaper, faster, and nobody has to agree on a time a week in advance.

07

Bottomless and boozy brunch in Boston: what the law allows

If you are coming from New York, Chicago or almost anywhere else, this is the part of Boston brunch that will surprise you. Massachusetts has some of the strictest alcohol pricing rules in the country. Since 1984, state regulations have banned bars and restaurants from offering free drinks, unlimited drinks for a fixed price, discounted drinks for a limited time, or more than one drink to a person at a time. That is why Massachusetts has no happy hour, and it is why a true "bottomless mimosa" brunch is essentially nonexistent in Boston.

What you will find instead: restaurants with good brunch cocktail lists where you pay per drink, at the same price all day. Some places offer a brunch prix fixe that includes one cocktail, which is legal because the drink is priced at its regular rate as part of the meal. A few advertise "mimosa flights" or carafes, which are again just drinks sold at their normal price. If a place in Massachusetts advertises bottomless anything alcoholic, be skeptical; the state's licensing board has fined restaurants for exactly that.

For a boozy brunch that works within the rules, the best bets on this list are Buttermilk & Bourbon (a long cocktail list and a party atmosphere), Stephi's on Tremont (a serious Bloody Mary), Lulu's Allston (cheap cocktails, young crowd) and The Friendly Toast (a dedicated Bloody Mary menu). 08

Weekday brunch in Boston

Boston is a city of early risers and late-morning meetings, and several of the best brunches on this list are available every day of the week with no wait. If you are visiting midweek, or you work remotely and want a Tuesday brunch, these are the ones to know.

All-day, every day: Trident (Newbury St), Zaftigs (Brookline), Cafe Landwer (both locations), The Friendly Toast (both locations) and Tatte (most locations) serve the full breakfast menu from morning until at least mid-afternoon on weekdays. Paramount serves breakfast daily and is a completely different, calmer restaurant on a Wednesday morning than on a Sunday.

Bakery cafes: Flour, Sofra and Tatte open early on weekdays and are at their best before 10am, when the pastries are fresh and the laptops have not yet colonised the tables. Mike & Patty's is a weekday breakfast-sandwich shop as much as a weekend brunch spot.

Weekend only: Buttermilk & Bourbon, Stephi's, Lulu's, Loyal Nine and Henrietta's Table run brunch on Saturday and Sunday only (Henrietta's buffet is Sunday only). Check each restaurant's site, because brunch days occasionally expand around holidays. If you are looking for a place to work after a weekday brunch, our guide to remote-work cafes in Boston overlaps with this list quite a bit.

How to beat the brunch wait in Boston

Go early or go late. The wait at nearly every walk-in brunch spot in Boston follows the same curve: nothing before 9:30am, a wall from 10:30am to 12:30pm, and a quick fade after 1:30pm. If you can be at Paramount, Zaftigs or North Street Grille by 9am you will walk in. If you cannot, aim for after 1:30pm and treat it as a late lunch.

Saturday over Sunday. Sunday is the bigger brunch day in Boston by a wide margin. The same restaurant at the same hour on Saturday is usually 20 to 30 minutes faster.

Put your name in and walk. Most spots that do not take reservations will take your name and phone number and text you. Beacon Hill, the South End and the North End are all good neighborhoods to kill 40 minutes in; the Public Garden is two blocks from Paramount and Mike & Patty's.

Use the counter-service places as a release valve. If the wait at a sit-down spot is over 45 minutes, there is almost always a Tatte or Flour within ten minutes' walk with a ten-minute wait and comparable food. Sofra, Mike & Patty's and Flour Fort Point are the cheapest and fastest brunches in this guide for exactly that reason.

Watch for game days and graduation weekends. Fenway-area brunch (Landwer on Beacon Street, Eventide's neighbors) is packed before Red Sox day games. Harvard Square and Kendall brunches are booked solid on commencement weekends in late May. Marathon Monday in April turns Back Bay brunch into a crowd-control exercise; go to Cambridge or Brookline instead.

FAQ

Boston brunch: common questions

Is there bottomless brunch in Boston?

Almost never. Massachusetts law bans free drinks, unlimited drinks for a fixed price and discounted drink specials, which is why the state has no happy hour and why true bottomless mimosas are rare to nonexistent in Boston. Some restaurants offer a brunch prix fixe that includes one cocktail at regular price, and many have good brunch cocktail lists, but you pay per drink.

What time does brunch start in Boston?

Most Boston brunch spots open between 8am and 10am on weekends and serve brunch until 2pm or 3pm. Bakery cafes like Tatte, Flour and Sofra open earlier, around 7am to 8am, and all-day places like Trident and Friendly Toast serve breakfast food until evening.

How do I avoid the brunch wait in Boston?

Arrive before 9:30am, go on a Saturday instead of a Sunday, or go on a weekday. Most of the most popular spots do not take weekend reservations, so put your name in and walk. Bakery cafes and counter-service places move much faster than sit-down brunch restaurants.

Where is the best cheap brunch in Boston?

Flour Bakery, Mike and Patty's, Sofra and Tatte all serve a filling breakfast for roughly $10 to $18. Paramount on Beacon Hill and Zaftigs in Brookline are the best value for a full sit-down plate. See the cheap brunch section of this guide for details.

Which Boston brunch spots take reservations?

Henrietta's Table, Buttermilk and Bourbon, Stephi's on Tremont, Cafe Landwer and Lulu's Allston generally take brunch reservations. Paramount, Mike and Patty's, North Street Grille, Zaftigs and the bakery cafes are walk-in only or mostly walk-in. Policies change, so check the restaurant's site before you go.

Where should I go for brunch with a big group in Boston?

Henrietta's Table in Harvard Square, Buttermilk and Bourbon in Back Bay and Friendly Toast in Back Bay or Kendall Square handle groups of six or more best, because they have large rooms and take reservations. Zaftigs in Brookline seats big tables too but the wait can be long on Sundays.
