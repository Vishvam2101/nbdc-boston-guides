There are more free things to do in Boston than almost any city its size, because the best parts of it were never built to sell tickets. The Freedom Trail is a painted line on the sidewalk. The Charles River Esplanade, the Harborwalk, Boston Common and the Public Garden are public parks. Several of the city's top museums have a free evening every week, and the most beautiful room in town is a public library reading room.

This guide covers 27 free things to do in Boston, grouped by type: the essential sights, free museum days, views and waterfront walks, neighborhoods worth exploring on foot, free things to do at night, and what changes by season. Every entry includes the address, the nearest T stop, hours where they matter, and a tip you would only get from someone who lives here.

We update it as museum schedules and event dates change. Everything here is free to do; where a site charges to go inside, we say so, and the outside or the walk is still free.

01

The best free things to do in Boston

If you only have one day, start here. These are the free Boston attractions that locals recommend first and visitors remember most.

Walk the Freedom Trail

Starts at Boston Common, 139 Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)2.5 miles, 2–3 hoursFree; some sites charge inside

The Freedom Trail is a 2.5-mile red brick line that connects 16 historic sites, from Boston Common through Downtown and the North End to the USS Constitution in Charlestown. Walking it is free and self-guided. Highlights along the way include the Massachusetts State House, the Granary Burying Ground where Samuel Adams and Paul Revere are buried, the Old State House, Faneuil Hall, Paul Revere's House and the Old North Church. A few sites charge a small admission to go inside, but the exteriors and most of the graveyards are free.

Local tip: start around 8am. You will have the Downtown stretch to yourself and reach the North End as the bakeries open. Full stop-by-stop route in our Freedom Trail guide.

Watch the sunset from the Charles River Esplanade

Access from Arlington St or the Fiedler FootbridgeCharles/MGH (Red)Open dawn to dusk, free

The Esplanade is a three-mile park along the Boston side of the Charles River, facing west toward Cambridge, which means it gets the sunset every evening. The dock near the Hatch Shell is the best spot. In summer you'll see sailboats from Community Boating, rowers, runners and half the city on blankets. The Citgo sign lights up behind you as it gets dark.

Visit the Boston Public Library and Bates Hall

700 Boylston St, Copley SquareCopley (Green)Mon–Thu 9–8, Fri–Sat 9–5, Sun 11–5Free

The McKim Building of the Boston Public Library opened in 1895 and is one of the most beautiful public buildings in the country. Bates Hall, the main reading room, has a 50-foot barrel-vaulted ceiling, long oak tables and green lamps, and anyone can sit and read or work there. Don't miss the John Singer Sargent murals on the third floor and the central courtyard, which is one of the quietest places in Back Bay. Free art and architecture tours run most days.

Boston Common, the Public Garden and the Ducklings

Between Beacon, Charles, Boylston and Arlington StreetsArlington or Park Street (Green)Always open, free

Boston Common, founded in 1634, is the oldest public park in the United States. The Public Garden next to it is the prettier of the two, with the lagoon, the weeping willows and the bronze Make Way for Ducklings statues near the Charles and Beacon Street corner. Locals dress the ducklings for holidays and Bruins playoff games. The Swan Boats cost a few dollars in season, but walking the garden is free year-round.

Acorn Street and a walk through Beacon Hill

Acorn St, between West Cedar and WillowCharles/MGH (Red)Public street, free

Acorn Street is a short cobblestone lane in Beacon Hill that is often called the most photographed street in America. Beacon Hill itself is the real attraction: brick sidewalks, working gas lamps, Louisburg Square, and Charles Street for antique shops and cafés. The whole neighborhood is about 15 minutes to walk end to end. Go on a weekday evening for the fewest people and the best light.

Free museums in Boston (and when to go)

ICA Boston on a free Thursday night

25 Harbor Shore Dr, SeaportCourthouse (Silver Line SL1/SL2)Free Thu 5–9pm

The Institute of Contemporary Art sits in a glass building cantilevered over Boston Harbor. Free Thursday nights are busy and social, especially in summer when the harbor deck is open. The fourth-floor Founders Gallery has a floor-to-ceiling view of the water that is worth the visit even if contemporary art isn't your thing.

Harvard Yard and the Harvard Art Museums

32 Quincy St, CambridgeHarvard (Red)Museums Tue–Sun 10–5, free

The Harvard Art Museums have been free to everyone since 2023. Three museums share one building with a Renzo Piano glass roof: European and American art, Germanic and Bauhaus works, and Asian and Islamic art. Combine it with a walk through Harvard Yard, the Widener Library steps and the bookstores of Harvard Square for a full free afternoon in Cambridge.

Museum of Fine Arts on a Wednesday evening

465 Huntington Ave, FenwayMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)Wed after 4pm, pay what you wish

The MFA is one of the largest art museums in the country, and Wednesday evenings are voluntary admission. Four hours is not enough for the whole museum, so pick a wing: the Art of the Americas for Copley and Sargent, the Japanese garden and Buddhist temple room, or the Egyptian galleries.

03

Free views, parks and waterfront walks

Boston is a harbor city with a river running through it, and nearly all of the waterfront is public. These are the best free views of Boston and the walks that connect them.

Walk the Boston Harborwalk

Best stretch: ICA to Christopher Columbus ParkCourthouse (Silver) to Aquarium (Blue)About 2 miles

The Harborwalk is a 43-mile public path along Boston's waterfront. The essential section runs from the ICA in the Seaport, past Fan Pier and the Moakley Courthouse, over the Seaport Boulevard bridge, around Long Wharf and the Aquarium to Christopher Columbus Park at the edge of the North End. Do it around dusk for the skyline and harbor lights.

See the skyline from Piers Park in East Boston

95 Marginal St, East BostonMaverick (Blue), 10-minute walkOpen daily, free

The best free view of the Boston skyline is across the harbor in East Boston. Piers Park has a long pier, lawns and a direct line of sight to Downtown, the Custom House tower and the Zakim Bridge. It is quiet, rarely crowded, and one Blue Line stop from Downtown. LoPresti Park a few blocks north has a similar view with even fewer people.

Castle Island and Fort Independence

2010 William J Day Blvd, South BostonBus 9 or 11 from Broadway (Red)Park open daily; fort tours summer weekends

Castle Island is a park on a causeway in South Boston with a 19th-century granite fort, a beach, and a two-mile loop around Pleasure Bay. Free guided tours of Fort Independence run on summer weekends. Planes landing at Logan pass directly overhead, and Sullivan's, the takeout stand by the parking lot, has been selling cheap hot dogs since 1951.

Arnold Arboretum

125 Arborway, Jamaica PlainForest Hills (Orange)Open dawn to dusk, free

The Arnold Arboretum is a 281-acre botanical collection run by Harvard and open to the public for free every day. Peters Hill at the southern end has a clear view of the Downtown skyline. Lilac Sunday, on the second Sunday in May, is the one day of the year picnicking is allowed and draws tens of thousands of people.

Bunker Hill Monument and the USS Constitution

Monument Square, CharlestownCommunity College (Orange) or the Long Wharf ferryMonument climb free, seasonal hours

Climbing the 294 steps of the Bunker Hill Monument is free and gives you a view over Charlestown, the harbor and Downtown. A ten-minute walk away, the USS Constitution is the oldest commissioned warship afloat in the world and is free to board (bring a photo ID). Both are at the end of the Freedom Trail.

Longfellow Bridge at night

Between Charles/MGH and Kendall SquareCharles/MGH (Red)Public, always open

The Longfellow Bridge, nicknamed the Salt-and-Pepper Bridge for its towers, connects Beacon Hill to Cambridge. Walking it after dark gives you the Back Bay skyline reflected in the river and Red Line trains passing beside you. It is a 15-minute round trip and one of the most underrated free things to do in Boston at night.

04

Boston neighborhoods worth exploring on foot

Boston is compact, and its neighborhoods are distinct enough that walking between them feels like changing cities. These cost nothing unless you stop to eat, which you probably will.

The North End

Hanover St and the streets off itHaymarket (Orange/Green)Best in the evening

Boston's oldest residential neighborhood and its Italian district, with nearly 100 restaurants in under half a square mile. Hanover Street is the main drag; Salem Street and Prince Street are quieter and more local. The Paul Revere House, the Old North Church and Copp's Hill Burying Ground are all here on the Freedom Trail. Our full North End guide covers where to eat and the cannoli question.

Faneuil Hall, Quincy Market and Haymarket

4 S Market StGovernment Center (Blue/Green) or HaymarketHaymarket produce market Fri–Sat

Faneuil Hall has been a market and meeting hall since 1742, and the Great Hall upstairs is free to visit with National Park Service rangers giving short talks. Quincy Market next door is mostly food stalls, but the street performers outside are free and often very good. On Fridays and Saturdays, Haymarket, the open-air produce market a block away, is the cheapest place to buy fruit in the city.

Harvard Square, Cambridge

Around Harvard stationHarvard (Red)Lively until late

Harvard Square has bookstores open until 11pm, street musicians, the steps of Widener Library, and the Charles River a five-minute walk south. It is the easiest free evening in Greater Boston. The Harvard Book Store and Grolier Poetry Book Shop are worth a browse; the Harvard Art Museums (free) are two blocks east.

SoWa Open Market in the South End

460 Harrison AveTufts Medical Center (Orange), 12-minute walkSundays 11–4, May–October

SoWa is a Sunday market in the South End with about 200 vendors, a farmers market, food trucks and a vintage market in a former power station. It is free to walk around, and the South End's brownstone streets and Union Park are a pleasant walk from Back Bay.

Back Bay and Newbury Street

Newbury St between Arlington and Mass AveArlington, Copley or Hynes (Green)Shops 10–7 most days

Back Bay is Boston's grid of Victorian brownstones, laid out in the 1800s on filled-in marshland. Commonwealth Avenue Mall is a tree-lined walk down the middle of it. Newbury Street is the shopping street, but window shopping and people-watching from the benches are free. Trinity Church and the library face each other across Copley Square at the eastern end.

05

Free things to do in Boston at night

Free concerts and movies at the Hatch Shell

47 David G Mugar Way, EsplanadeCharles/MGH (Red)Summer Wed and Fri evenings

The Hatch Memorial Shell on the Esplanade hosts free events all summer: Boston Landmarks Orchestra concerts on Wednesday nights, free Friday movies, and the Boston Pops Fourth of July concert and fireworks. Bring a blanket and arrive early for the big events.

The skyline from Fan Pier

Fan Pier Park, SeaportCourthouse (Silver)Always open

Fan Pier is the small park in front of the Moakley Courthouse in the Seaport, and it faces Downtown across Fort Point Channel. After dark it is the best close-up view of the lit-up skyline in the city, and it is a short walk from the ICA and the Harborwalk.

Free trivia nights

Bars across Allston, Somerville and CambridgeMost weeknightsFree to play

Dozens of Boston bars run free pub trivia on weeknights, and most will add a solo player to an existing team if you ask. You pay for your drink, not the game. We keep a schedule in the best trivia nights in Boston.

Boston Common in December

Boston Common, near Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)Tree lighting early December

The Boston Common Christmas tree, a gift from Nova Scotia every year since 1971, is lit in early December with free music and fireworks. The Frog Pond skating rink charges admission, but watching from the Brewer Fountain area with a hot chocolate is free.

Walk the Harvard Bridge and count the Smoots

Massachusetts Ave over the CharlesHynes (Green) or Kendall (Red)Public, always open

The Harvard Bridge, which carries Mass Ave from Back Bay to MIT, is marked every 5.5 feet in "Smoots," a unit invented by MIT students in 1958. The full bridge is 364.4 Smoots plus one ear. It is a ten-minute walk with a view of the river in both directions and a good way home from Cambridge at night.

07

Getting around Boston for free (or close to it)

Boston is small enough that most of this list is walkable. Downtown to the North End is 10 minutes on foot, Downtown to the Seaport is 15, and Downtown to Copley Square in Back Bay is 20. If you do need transit, the MBTA subway (the T) is $2.40 per ride with a CharlieCard or contactless card, and a 7-day pass is $22.50. A few bus routes are free: the 23, 28 and 29 through Roxbury, Dorchester and Mattapan have had no fare since 2022.

The MBTA ferry from Long Wharf to the Charlestown Navy Yard runs about every 30 minutes and costs the same as a subway ride, which makes it the cheapest harbor cruise in Boston and a good way to get to the USS Constitution. Bluebikes, the city's bike share, is $2.95 for a single ride and often has free-ride days sponsored by the city; the Esplanade and the Southwest Corridor are the best car-free routes.

FAQ

Free things to do in Boston: common questions

What is the best free thing to do in Boston?

Walking the Freedom Trail. It is 2.5 miles, self-guided, and passes 16 historic sites including Boston Common, the Old State House, Paul Revere's House and the Old North Church. Most people take two to three hours. If you only have an hour, walk the section from Boston Common to Faneuil Hall.

Which Boston museums are free?

The Harvard Art Museums are free every day. The ICA is free Thursdays 5–9pm. The MFA is pay-what-you-wish Wednesdays after 4pm. The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum is free on your birthday, and free forever if your name is Isabella. The MIT Museum is free on the last Sunday of each month.

Is Boston walkable?

Yes. Boston is consistently ranked among the most walkable cities in the US. Downtown, the North End, Beacon Hill, Back Bay and the Seaport are all within a 30 to 40 minute walk of each other, and most of the free attractions on this page are in those neighborhoods or on the waterfront.

Is there free public transit in Boston?

The subway costs $2.40 per ride, but the 23, 28 and 29 bus routes are fare-free. The Charlestown ferry from Long Wharf costs the same as a subway ride and doubles as a harbor cruise. Bluebikes has occasional free-ride days.

What can you do in Boston for free in winter?

Indoor options include the Boston Public Library, the free museum nights, Faneuil Hall's Great Hall, and the connected walkways through the Prudential Center and Copley Place. Outdoors, the tree lighting on Boston Common and the holiday lights on Commonwealth Avenue are free in December.

What free things are there to do in Boston with kids?

The Make Way for Ducklings statues and the Public Garden, the Boston Common playground and Frog Pond spray pool in summer, the USS Constitution, Castle Island beach, the Harborwalk, and free story times at the Boston Public Library. Our family guide has more.

What is free to do in Boston this weekend?

Every weekend: the Freedom Trail, the parks and waterfront, Haymarket on Friday and Saturday, and the free museum days above. From May to October, SoWa Open Market on Sunday. We update things to do in Boston this weekend every Thursday with events.
