The trouble with things to do in Boston is not finding them, it is that the good ones are so close together you can do four before lunch and feel like you have done nothing. The Freedom Trail runs through three neighborhoods. The Public Garden is across the street from Beacon Hill, which is across the river from Cambridge, which is a 20-minute walk down the Esplanade from the museums. Boston rewards a plan, and punishes a list.

This hub is where all of our things-to-do guides live. At the top are 26 guides sorted by how you are actually going to use them: free, at night, this weekend, on a date, in the rain, alone, on a budget, and by season. Below that is a ranked list of the 20 essential Boston activities, the ones a local would tell you about first, each with an address and the nearest T stop.

If you only have a day, do the first five on the list in order. They are all within a 40-minute walk of each other, and you will end in the North End at dinner time, which is the right place to end.

20 essential things to do in Boston

Ranked roughly in the order a local would suggest them to a first-time visitor. The first ten are walkable from each other; the second ten need the T or a ferry.

Walk the Freedom Trail

Starts at Boston Common, 139 Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)2.5 miles, 2–3 hoursFree; some sites charge inside

A red brick line through 16 historic sites, from the Common and the State House through the Old State House, Faneuil Hall, Paul Revere's House and the Old North Church to the USS Constitution in Charlestown. It is the single best introduction to the city. Go early, and use our stop-by-stop guide.

Eat your way through the North End

Hanover St and the streets off itHaymarket (Orange/Green)Best from 5pm

Boston's Italian neighborhood has close to 100 restaurants in half a square mile. Dinner on Hanover or Salem Street, then a cannoli from Mike's or Modern (locals argue; try both). Our North End guide has the shortlist.

The Public Garden, Boston Common and Beacon Hill

Between Beacon, Charles, Boylston and Arlington StreetsArlington (Green) or Park StreetAlways open, free

The oldest public park in the country and the prettiest one next door, with the lagoon, the Swan Boats in season and the Make Way for Ducklings statues. Cross Beacon Street into Beacon Hill for gas lamps, brick sidewalks, Acorn Street and Charles Street cafés.

Fenway Park, for a game or a tour

4 Jersey StKenmore (Green B/C/D) or Fenway (Green D)Games Apr–Oct; tours year-roundTours around $25; check before you go

The oldest ballpark in Major League Baseball, opened in 1912, and still the best atmosphere in the sport. Bleacher seats are the cheap way in; the hour-long tour runs daily and includes the Green Monster. Jersey Street outside is closed to cars on game days and full of sausage carts.

Museum of Fine Arts

465 Huntington AveMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)Closed Tuesdays; Wed after 4pm pay what you wish

One of the largest art museums in the country. Pick a wing: Art of the Americas for Copley and Sargent, the Japanese galleries and Buddhist temple room, or the Egyptian collection. The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum, a Venetian palace built around a courtyard, is a five-minute walk away.

Sunset on the Charles River Esplanade

Access from Arlington St via the Fiedler FootbridgeCharles/MGH (Red)Dawn to dusk, free

Three miles of park along the river, facing west toward Cambridge, which means it gets every sunset. The dock by the Hatch Shell is the spot. In summer there are free concerts and movies at the Shell; in any season it is the best walk in the city.

Harvard Square and Harvard Yard

Around Harvard station, CambridgeHarvard (Red)Yard always open; Harvard Art Museums free, Tue–Sun 10–5

Walk the Yard, touch the John Harvard statue's foot if you must, then the free Harvard Art Museums on Quincy Street and the bookstores around the square. Our Harvard Square guide covers where to eat.

The ICA and the Seaport Harborwalk

25 Harbor Shore DrCourthouse (Silver Line SL1/SL2)Free Thursdays 5–9pm

The Institute of Contemporary Art sits in a glass box over the harbor. Come for the free Thursday night, then walk the Harborwalk past Fan Pier to the Seaport Boulevard bridge for the best close-up skyline view in the city. The Seaport has most of the rooftop bars, too.

Get on the harbor

Long Wharf, 1 Long WharfAquarium (Blue)Ferries May–Oct; MBTA Charlestown ferry year-round

Three options: a proper harbor cruise, the seasonal ferry to Spectacle or Georges Island in the Boston Harbor Islands, or the MBTA ferry to Charlestown, which costs the same as a subway ride and takes 10 minutes. All three leave from Long Wharf next to the Aquarium.

Boston Public Library and Copley Square

700 Boylston StCopley (Green)Mon–Thu 9–8, Fri–Sat 9–5, Sun 11–5Free

Bates Hall, the 1895 reading room with the barrel-vaulted ceiling and green lamps, is open to anyone. The courtyard is the quietest spot in Back Bay, and Trinity Church faces it across Copley Square. Newbury Street starts a block north.

Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum

25 Evans WayMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)Closed TuesdaysFree on your birthday; otherwise check before you go

A Venetian-style palazzo built by one eccentric collector around a flowering courtyard, still hung exactly as she left it, empty frames from the 1990 heist and all. Smaller and stranger than the MFA, and many locals prefer it.

Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market

4 S Market StGovernment Center (Blue/Green)Market open daily

A meeting hall since 1742, a food hall since 1826. The Great Hall upstairs is free with park rangers on hand. Quincy Market is touristy but the street performers outside are often excellent, and Haymarket's open-air produce stalls run Friday and Saturday a block away.

Museum of Science

1 Science ParkScience Park/West End (Green)Open daily; check before you go for hours

Built on a dam across the Charles, with a planetarium, an IMAX dome, a live lightning show and a view of both Boston and Cambridge from the cafeteria. The best rainy-day option with kids, and a solid one without.

New England Aquarium and the Harborwalk

1 Central WharfAquarium (Blue)Open daily; timed tickets

A four-story ocean tank with a spiral ramp around it, penguins on the ground floor, and harbor seals outside that you can watch for free. Walk the Harborwalk north from here to Christopher Columbus Park and into the North End.

Bunker Hill Monument and the USS Constitution

Monument Square, CharlestownCommunity College (Orange) or the Long Wharf ferryMonument climb free, seasonal hours; ship free with ID

The end of the Freedom Trail. Climb 294 steps for a view over the harbor and Downtown, then board the oldest commissioned warship still afloat. Charlestown's Monument Avenue is one of the prettiest streets in the city.

Skywalk or a rooftop bar for the view from above

Prudential Center, 800 Boylston StPrudential (Green E)View Boston: check hours and price before you go

View Boston, on the top three floors of the Prudential Tower, is the observation deck. The cheaper option is a drink at one of the rooftop bars in Back Bay or the Seaport at sunset. Both are covered in the best views of Boston.

Arnold Arboretum

125 Arborway, Jamaica PlainForest Hills (Orange)Dawn to dusk, free

A 281-acre Harvard-run botanical collection in Jamaica Plain, free every day, with a skyline view from Peters Hill and lilacs in May. Combine it with lunch on Centre Street in JP.

Castle Island

2010 William J Day Blvd, South BostonBus 9 or 11 from Broadway (Red)Park daily; fort tours summer weekends

A granite fort, a beach, a two-mile loop around Pleasure Bay, planes landing overhead, and Sullivan's for hot dogs since 1951. The most local afternoon on this list.

MIT and Kendall Square

77 Massachusetts Ave, CambridgeKendall/MIT (Red)Campus open; MIT Museum free last Sunday of the month

Walk the Infinite Corridor, find the Stata Center and the Great Dome, and see the MIT Museum's collection of robots and holograms. Then cross the Longfellow Bridge back to Boston at dusk for the best free skyline walk in town.

Somerville and Cambridge after dark

Davis Square, Union Square, Central SquareDavis or Central (Red), Union Square (Green)Evenings

Where the city actually goes out. Live music, the best dive bars and trivia nights, breweries and late-night food, across the river where the rent is (slightly) lower. See Boston at night.

FAQ

Things to do in Boston: common questions

What is the number one thing to do in Boston?

Walk the Freedom Trail. It is free, 2.5 miles, and passes 16 historic sites from Boston Common through Downtown and the North End to the USS Constitution. It doubles as a tour of the city's oldest neighborhoods, and you can eat in the North End at the end.

What can you do in Boston in one day?

Start on Beacon Hill, walk the Freedom Trail through Downtown to the North End, have lunch on Hanover Street, walk the Harborwalk to the Seaport and the ICA, then finish with sunset on the Esplanade or dinner in Back Bay. All of it is on foot. The one-day itinerary has timings.

What are the best things to do in Boston for free?

The Freedom Trail, the Public Garden, the Boston Public Library, the Esplanade, the Harborwalk, Harvard Yard and the Harvard Art Museums, Piers Park in East Boston, and free museum nights at the ICA on Thursdays and the MFA on Wednesdays. All 27 are in free things to do in Boston.

What is there to do in Boston when it rains?

The MFA, the Gardner, the ICA and the Museum of Science are full-day museums. The Boston Public Library, the Mapparium and the Prudential Center walkways are free and dry, and the North End and Chinatown are good for long lunches. See rainy day Boston.

Is Boston a good city for a weekend trip?

Yes. It is compact enough that two days covers the Freedom Trail, the North End, Beacon Hill, Back Bay, one or two museums and a harbor view, without a car. Trains from New York take about four hours and Logan Airport is 10 minutes from Downtown. Use the weekend itinerary.

What do locals do for fun in Boston?

Run or walk the Esplanade, go to Red Sox games, argue about pizza and cannoli, take the ferry to the harbor islands in summer, do trivia nights, and go to Somerville and Cambridge for bars and music. Winters mean museums, long dinners and the Bruins.
