20 essential things to do in Boston

The first ten are walkable from each other. The second ten need the T or a ferry.

Walk the Freedom Trail

Starts at Boston Common, 139 Tremont StPark Street (Red/Green)2.5 miles, 2–3 hoursFree; some sites charge

A red brick line through 16 historic sites, Common to USS Constitution. Go early and use our stop-by-stop guide.

Eat your way through the North End

Hanover St and aroundHaymarket (Orange/Green)Best from 5pm

Close to 100 Italian restaurants in half a square mile. Dinner, then a cannoli from Mike's or Modern. Shortlist in the North End guide.

The Public Garden, Boston Common and Beacon Hill

Between Beacon, Charles, Boylston and Arlington StreetsArlington (Green) or Park StreetAlways open, free

The country's oldest public park, Swan Boats in season and the Ducklings statues. Cross Beacon Street for gas lamps and Acorn Street.

Fenway Park, for a game or a tour

4 Jersey StKenmore (Green B/C/D)Games Apr–Oct; tours year-roundTours around $25

The oldest ballpark in the majors, opened 1912. Bleacher seats are the cheap way in; the tour includes the Green Monster.

Museum of Fine Arts

465 Huntington AveMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)Closed Tuesdays; Wed after 4pm pay what you wish

One of the largest art museums in the country. Pick one wing: Art of the Americas, Japanese or Egyptian.

Sunset on the Charles River Esplanade

Fiedler Footbridge from Arlington StCharles/MGH (Red)Dawn to dusk, free

Three miles of riverside park facing west, so it gets every sunset. The dock by the Hatch Shell is the spot.

Harvard Square and Harvard Yard

Around Harvard station, CambridgeHarvard (Red)Harvard Art Museums free, Tue–Sun 10–5

Walk the Yard, then the free Harvard Art Museums and the bookstores. Where to eat is in the Harvard Square guide.

The ICA and the Seaport Harborwalk

25 Harbor Shore DrCourthouse (Silver Line SL1/SL2)Free Thursdays 5–9pm

Contemporary art in a glass box over the harbor. Come Thursday night, then walk the Harborwalk for the best close-up skyline view.

Get on the harbor

Long WharfAquarium (Blue)Ferries May–Oct; Charlestown ferry year-round

A harbor cruise, the seasonal ferry to the harbor islands, or the MBTA ferry to Charlestown for the price of a subway ride.

Boston Public Library and Copley Square

700 Boylston StCopley (Green)Mon–Thu 9–8, Fri–Sat 9–5, Sun 11–5Free

Bates Hall, the 1895 reading room with green lamps, is open to anyone. The courtyard is the quietest spot in Back Bay.

Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum

25 Evans WayMuseum of Fine Arts (Green E)Closed TuesdaysFree on your birthday

A Venetian-style palazzo around a flowering courtyard, empty frames from the 1990 heist included. Many locals prefer it to the MFA.

Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market

4 S Market StGovernment Center (Blue/Green)Market open daily

A meeting hall since 1742, a food hall since 1826. The Great Hall upstairs is free. Haymarket's produce stalls run Friday and Saturday.

Museum of Science

1 Science ParkScience Park/West End (Green)Open daily; check hours

Built on a dam across the Charles, with a planetarium, IMAX dome and live lightning show. The best rainy-day option with kids.

New England Aquarium and the Harborwalk

1 Central WharfAquarium (Blue)Open daily; timed tickets

A four-story ocean tank, penguins downstairs, and harbor seals outside you can watch for free. Walk the Harborwalk north to the North End.

Bunker Hill Monument and the USS Constitution

Monument Square, CharlestownCommunity College (Orange) or ferryMonument climb free; ship free with ID

The end of the Freedom Trail. Climb 294 steps for the harbor view, then board the oldest commissioned warship still afloat.

Skywalk or a rooftop bar for the view from above

Prudential Center, 800 Boylston StPrudential (Green E)View Boston: check hours and price

View Boston is the observation deck atop the Prudential. Cheaper: a sunset drink at a rooftop bar. Both in best views of Boston.

Arnold Arboretum

125 Arborway, Jamaica PlainForest Hills (Orange)Dawn to dusk, free

281 acres of Harvard-run botanical collection, free every day, with a skyline view from Peters Hill and lilacs in May.

Castle Island

2010 William J Day Blvd, South BostonBus 9 or 11 from Broadway (Red)Park daily; fort tours summer weekends

A granite fort, a beach, a two-mile loop around Pleasure Bay, and Sullivan's for hot dogs since 1951. The most local afternoon on this list.

MIT and Kendall Square

77 Massachusetts Ave, CambridgeKendall/MIT (Red)MIT Museum free last Sunday of the month

Walk the Infinite Corridor and find the Great Dome, then cross the Longfellow Bridge back at dusk for the best free skyline walk.

Somerville and Cambridge after dark

Davis, Union and Central SquaresDavis or Central (Red), Union Square (Green)Evenings

Where the city goes out: live music, dive bars, trivia, breweries, late-night food. See Boston at night.

FAQ

Things to do in Boston: common questions

What is the number one thing to do in Boston?

Walk the Freedom Trail. It is free, 2.5 miles, passes 16 historic sites, and ends near the North End for dinner.

What can you do in Boston in one day?

Beacon Hill, the Freedom Trail to the North End, lunch on Hanover Street, the Harborwalk to the ICA, then sunset on the Esplanade. See the one-day itinerary.

What are the best things to do in Boston for free?

The Freedom Trail, Public Garden, library, Esplanade, Harborwalk, Harvard Yard, ICA Thursday nights and MFA Wednesday evenings. All 27 in free things to do.

What is there to do in Boston when it rains?

The MFA, the Gardner, the ICA and the Museum of Science. The library and the Prudential walkways are free and dry. See rainy day Boston.

Is Boston a good city for a weekend trip?

Yes. Two days covers the Freedom Trail, North End, Beacon Hill, Back Bay and a museum, no car needed. Use the weekend itinerary.
