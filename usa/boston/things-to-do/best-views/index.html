The best views of Boston.
16 places to see the skyline.

By no bad days club, BostonUpdated September 8, 202613 min read

Boston does not have a skyline you can see from inside Boston. The city is built on a small peninsula and the towers are clustered in two groups, Downtown and Back Bay, so from most streets you are looking up at buildings, not across at them. The best views of Boston are almost all from the edges: across the harbor from East Boston, across the Charles from Cambridge, from the Seaport looking back at Downtown, or from the few hills that survived the 19th-century habit of flattening them for landfill.

That is actually good news, because the edges are cheap. Of the 16 viewpoints in this guide, 13 are free, and most of them are public parks or bridges you can reach on the T in under 20 minutes from Downtown. The paid ones, the View Boston observation deck at the Prudential and a couple of rooftop bars, are worth it for height, but they are the exception.

For each place we give the address, the nearest T stop, whether it costs anything, and the time of day it is actually best, because a view that is flat and hazy at noon can be the best thing you see all trip at 7pm. Sections cover free views, paid views, sunset, night, the water, the hills, and a table with everything in one place.

The short answer

01

The best free views of Boston

These are public parks, sidewalks and bridges. No ticket, no drink minimum, and most are open around the clock.

Piers Park, East Boston

95 Marginal St, East BostonMaverick (Blue), 10-minute walkDaily, dawn to about 10pmFreeBest at sunset

Piers Park is the single best free view of the Boston skyline, and most visitors never hear about it. It sits on a former shipping pier directly across the inner harbor from Downtown, so the whole waterfront is laid out in front of you: the Custom House tower, the Financial District, the Zakim Bridge to the right, the Seaport to the left, and the planes coming into Logan behind you. There is a long promenade, lawns, a playground and benches facing the water. Go an hour before sunset; the sun drops behind the Downtown towers and the water goes orange, then the buildings light up.

Local tip: LoPresti Park, at 33 Sumner Street about five minutes north along the water, has nearly the same view with a small beach and even fewer people. Do both; the Harborwalk connects them.

LoPresti Park, East Boston

33 Sumner St, East BostonMaverick (Blue), 5-minute walkDaily, dawn to duskFreeBest late afternoon

LoPresti is smaller and rougher than Piers Park and closer to Maverick station, with a pocket beach, a basketball court and a straight-on view of the North End waterfront and the Downtown skyline behind it. The angle is a little more northerly than Piers Park, so you get the Zakim Bridge and the TD Garden area more clearly. It is the quieter of the two on a summer evening.

Fan Pier Park, Seaport

Fan Pier Park, Northern Ave at the Moakley CourthouseCourthouse (Silver Line SL1/SL2)Always openFreeBest after dark

Fan Pier is the closest you can stand to the Downtown skyline and still see all of it. The park wraps around the Moakley Courthouse at the tip of the Seaport and looks back across Fort Point Channel at the Financial District towers, the Custom House, and the harbor opening out to the right. Because it is so close, it is best after dark, when the buildings are lit and the water reflects them. It is a short walk from the ICA and the Harborwalk, and the benches along the water are rarely full.

Longfellow Bridge

Cambridge St over the Charles, between Charles/MGH and Kendall SquareCharles/MGH (Red)Always openFreeBest at sunset and after dark

The Longfellow, with its four stone towers that give it the nickname the Salt-and-Pepper Bridge, has the best two-way view in the city. Look upriver and the whole of Back Bay is lined up along the water: the Hancock tower, the Prudential, the Esplanade lawns, and the Citgo sign at the far end. Look downriver and you get the Zakim Bridge and the Museum of Science. The Red Line runs down the middle of the bridge, and the wide sidewalks on both sides are open to walkers. It is a 15-minute round trip from Charles/MGH station, and best when the sun is setting over Cambridge and the Back Bay glass goes pink.

Memorial Drive and the MIT side of the Charles

Memorial Dr between the Harvard Bridge and the Longfellow, CambridgeKendall/MIT (Red)Always openFreeBest at golden hour

The classic Boston postcard is shot from here: the Back Bay skyline reflected in the river with sailboats in front of it. The riverside path along Memorial Drive on the Cambridge side faces south-east across the Charles at the whole of Back Bay, and because the sun sets behind you, the towers are lit gold for the last hour of the day. The stretch in front of MIT, between the Harvard Bridge and the Longfellow, is the widest part of the river basin and the best angle. Community Boating's sails fill the water on summer evenings. On Sundays from late April to mid-November, Memorial Drive itself is closed to cars between the Eliot Bridge and Western Avenue, which makes the upper stretch a good bike ride too.

Weeks Footbridge, Cambridge

Over the Charles between Memorial Dr and Soldiers Field Rd, near Harvard's DeWolfe StHarvard (Red), 10-minute walkAlways openFreeBest at sunset, and during the Head of the Charles

The Weeks is a pedestrian-only brick bridge from 1927 connecting Harvard's main campus to the business school. It does not face the skyline; it faces the river, upstream toward the Eliot Bridge and downstream toward the city, with the Harvard boathouses on both banks and the Downtown towers small in the distance. It is the best view on the upper Charles, and on the third weekend in October it is the place to watch the Head of the Charles regatta, when hundreds of crews row under it in a day.

Harvard Bridge (Mass Ave)

Massachusetts Ave over the Charles, Back Bay to MITHynes (Green) or Kendall/MIT (Red)Always openFreeBest at sunset

The Harvard Bridge carries Massachusetts Avenue across the widest part of the river, and from the middle you get the Back Bay skyline on one side and MIT's dome on the other, with the whole river basin around you. It is marked every 5.5 feet in Smoots, a unit invented by MIT students in 1958 and repainted every year. The sunset here is a long, wide one, with the Longfellow and the Downtown towers in the distance to the east and the Boston University bridge to the west. Ten minutes to cross, and it is the natural way home from Cambridge on a summer night.

Castle Island and Pleasure Bay

2010 William J Day Blvd, South BostonBus 9 or 11 from Broadway (Red), or a 30-minute walk from AndrewPark daily, dawn to duskFreeBest mid-afternoon into evening

Castle Island is a park at the eastern tip of South Boston with a granite fort, a small beach and a two-mile loop around Pleasure Bay. The view is not the skyline, which is behind you, but the harbor: the Harbor Islands, ships coming into the port, the Logan runways directly across the water with planes landing every couple of minutes, and the Downtown towers if you turn around at the causeway. Locals walk the loop; Sullivan's, the takeout stand by the fort, has been there since 1951 and the hot dogs are cheap.

02

Paid views: the observation deck and rooftop bars

Boston has one true observation deck and a handful of rooftop bars with real views. If you want to see the city from above rather than across, these are the options.

View Boston at the Prudential Center

800 Boylston St, Prudential Tower, floors 50–52Prudential (Green E) or Back Bay (Orange)Daily, roughly 10am to 10pm; check current hoursPaid: about $30–35 adults, timed entryBest at sunset; go any time for the full 360

View Boston opened in 2023 on the top three floors of the Prudential Tower, replacing the old Skywalk Observatory, and it is the highest public viewpoint in the city at 52 floors and about 750 feet. The 52nd floor is a full 360-degree indoor gallery; the 51st has an open-air roof deck; the 50th has a bar and a large model of the city. From up here you can see everything on this list at once: the harbor and the islands, the Charles and Cambridge, Fenway Park, the whole of Back Bay laid out in its grid, and on a clear day the mountains of New Hampshire. Sunset slots sell out and cost more; a clear morning is cheaper and just as good for orientation on your first day. Buy timed tickets in advance and check the price, which changes by season and time of day.

Local tip: the Back Bay skyline is the one everyone photographs, but from View Boston you are inside it. The best skyline views of the city are still the free ones from across the water; View Boston is for the map-like view of everything.

Lookout Rooftop and Bar, the Envoy Hotel

70 Sleeper St, SeaportCourthouse (Silver Line) or South Station (Red), 8-minute walkAfternoon to late; seasonal hours, check before you goNo cover, but drinks are $15–20 and lines formBest at dusk into night

The Lookout is the rooftop bar on the Envoy Hotel at the mouth of Fort Point Channel, and it faces the Downtown skyline directly across the water, closer than anywhere except Fan Pier. It is the most popular rooftop in the city for exactly that reason, and on summer weekends there is a line to get up. In winter it becomes an igloo bar. Arrive before 6pm on a weekday for a table by the glass rail and stay through the lights coming on. Full rooftop list in the best rooftop bars in Boston.

Other rooftops with a real view

VariousSeaport, Downtown, Back BayEvenings; seasonalCost of a drink

Two more worth knowing. Contessa, at 3 Newbury Street on the roof of the Newbury Hotel, is an expensive Italian restaurant with a glass-walled roof and a view straight down the Public Garden toward Downtown, and is best at lunch on a clear day. Rooftop at Revere, on the Revere Hotel at 200 Stuart Street, is a seasonal pool bar with a wide view of the Back Bay towers from the south and a more relaxed door than the Lookout. Both are drink-price-only; neither charges a cover.

03

The best sunset spots in Boston

Boston's sunsets happen over Cambridge, which means the west-facing side of the Charles River gets the sun and the east-facing side gets the light. Where you stand depends on which you want.

For the sun itself: the Charles River Esplanade on the Boston side, particularly the dock and the lawn by the Hatch Shell (access from Charles/MGH or the Fiedler Footbridge at Arlington Street). You are facing west up the river with Cambridge low on the far bank, and the sun goes down over the water with sailboats in front of it. The Longfellow Bridge does the same from higher up. The Harvard Bridge is the widest version.

For the skyline lit up: cross to the Cambridge side. From Memorial Drive in front of MIT, the sun is behind you and every window in Back Bay catches it. The last twenty minutes before sunset are the ones to be there for, and then stay another twenty for the blue hour when the lights come on and the river goes still.

For the skyline with the sun behind it: Piers Park in East Boston. The sun sets behind Downtown, so the towers silhouette against orange, and then the whole waterfront lights up as the sky darkens. It is the most dramatic of the three and the least crowded.

The overhead option: View Boston's sunset slots on the open-air 51st-floor deck. They sell out in summer and cost more than daytime tickets.

04

The Boston skyline at night

Boston's skyline is better after dark than most cities its size because so much of it is on water. Every lit tower gets a second copy in the harbor or the river, and the Zakim Bridge, lit blue, anchors the northern end of every view.

Fan Pier in the Seaport is the closest night view, with the Financial District a few hundred yards across the channel and the Custom House clock lit in the middle. It is safe, well lit and a short walk from the Seaport restaurants. The Longfellow Bridge gives you Back Bay reflected in the Charles, with Red Line trains crossing beside you; the walk from Charles/MGH to the Cambridge side and back takes 20 minutes and is one of the best free things to do in Boston at night. Piers Park is the widest night view, but the walk back to Maverick is through residential streets, which are fine, just quieter.

For a night view from height, View Boston stays open into the evening, and the Lookout rooftop faces Downtown directly. The Charlestown ferry runs into the evening too, and the last sailings give you the skyline from the water with almost nobody aboard. More night ideas in things to do in Boston at night.

05

Views of Boston from the water

Boston was built to be arrived at by sea, and the skyline was designed, by accident, to be seen from the harbor. These are the ways to get out on the water without booking a cruise.

The MBTA Charlestown ferry

Long Wharf, 1 Long Wharf, to Charlestown Navy Yard pierAquarium (Blue) at Long WharfRoughly every 30 min, morning to evening; check scheduleSame as a subway fare, about $2.40Best late afternoon

The F4 ferry between Long Wharf and the Charlestown Navy Yard is a commuter route that happens to be the cheapest harbor cruise in the city. It takes about 10 minutes, and on the way you pass the North End waterfront, get the full Downtown skyline from the water, and come in under the Zakim Bridge to dock next to the USS Constitution. Ride it as a loop, or use it to get back from the end of the Freedom Trail. Stand on the open stern deck for the view.

Spectacle Island, Boston Harbor Islands

Ferry from Long Wharf North, 66 Long WharfAquarium (Blue)Seasonal, roughly May to October; check scheduleFerry about $25–30 round trip; island freeBest on a clear summer afternoon

Spectacle Island is 20 minutes by ferry from Long Wharf and has the highest point in the Harbor Islands, a 157-foot hill built from the dirt dug out for the Big Dig. From the top you get the entire Boston skyline across four miles of open water, with the other islands scattered around you and the airport to the north. There is a beach, a visitor center and five miles of trails; bring your own food. Ferries run several times a day in summer and less often in spring and fall. It is the best long-range view of the city there is, and a full afternoon out.

The Harborwalk, Seaport to the North End

From the ICA, 25 Harbor Shore Dr, to Christopher Columbus ParkCourthouse (Silver) to Aquarium (Blue)Always openFreeBest at dusk

Not on the water, but along every inch of it. The two-mile stretch of the Harborwalk from the ICA in the Seaport, around Fan Pier, over the Seaport Boulevard bridge and past Long Wharf to the North End gives you the skyline from a dozen angles as you round the channel. Do it at dusk, ending at Christopher Columbus Park with the North End behind you, and you will have seen the lights come on from every side. It connects three other entries on this page: Fan Pier, the Lookout and the ferry.

06

Hills and high points

Boston flattened most of its hills to fill the harbor, but a few survived, and each gives a different, longer view of the city than the waterfront does.

Bunker Hill Monument, Charlestown

Monument Square, CharlestownCommunity College (Orange), 10-minute walk, or the Long Wharf ferryGrounds always open; climb roughly 10–5, seasonal; check before you goFreeBest in the morning

The 221-foot obelisk on Breed's Hill has 294 stone steps and no elevator, and the reward at the top is four small windows looking over Charlestown's rooftops, the Navy Yard, the Zakim Bridge and Downtown beyond. It is the only free high viewpoint in the city. Go in the morning when the climb is open and the line is short; on summer afternoons you can wait. The lawn outside is a good view on its own, and the last stop of the Freedom Trail.

Copp's Hill Burying Ground, North End

Hull St at Snow Hill St, North EndNorth Station (Orange/Green), 10-minute walkDaily, roughly 9–5FreeBest late morning

The highest point in the North End is a 17th-century burying ground, and from the northern fence you look across the mouth of the Charles at the Navy Yard, the USS Constitution's masts and the Bunker Hill Monument, with the Zakim Bridge to the left. It is a low view rather than a high one, but a specific one, and the headstones are the best colonial carving in the city.

Dorchester Heights, South Boston

Thomas Park, South BostonBroadway (Red), 15-minute uphill walk, or bus 9/11Park always openFreeBest late afternoon

Dorchester Heights is where Washington's cannon, dragged from Fort Ticonderoga, were set up in March 1776 and persuaded the British to leave Boston. The hill is now a small park in South Boston with a white marble tower on top and a view across the Fort Point Channel at the Downtown skyline from the south, which is an angle you do not get anywhere else. It is a quiet neighborhood park with almost no visitors, and the walk up from Broadway station goes through the heart of South Boston.

Peters Hill, Arnold Arboretum

Arnold Arboretum, Peters Hill gate on Bussey St, Jamaica PlainForest Hills (Orange), 10-minute walkDawn to duskFreeBest late afternoon; October for the trees

The Arboretum is a 281-acre Harvard botanical collection that is free to walk, and Peters Hill at its southern end is the highest point in it, at 240 feet. From the top you look north over six miles of trees and rooftops to the Downtown and Back Bay skylines standing up small on the horizon. It is a long view rather than a close one, and in October, with the collection in color around you, it is one of the best in the state. The whole Arboretum is worth the trip, and Forest Hills is 20 minutes from Downtown on the Orange Line.

Millennium Park, West Roxbury

300 Gardner St, West RoxburyCommuter Rail to West Roxbury, then a 20-minute walk; easier by car or bikeDawn to duskFreeBest at sunset

Millennium Park is a former landfill turned into 100 acres of open hilltop at the far south-western edge of the city, where the Charles River makes its last bend before leaving Boston. From the top of the hill the Downtown skyline is eight miles off and small, but the view is the widest in the city, with the Blue Hills to the south and the river meadows below. It is not easy to reach by T, which is why it is almost always empty. Worth it if you have a bike, a car or a long afternoon, and it is the best sunset in the city that nobody goes to.

08

How to plan a day of views

The viewpoints on this list cluster into three areas, and each makes a half day. The harbor: take the Blue Line to Maverick for Piers Park and LoPresti, ride back to Aquarium, and walk the Harborwalk around to Fan Pier for the lights, with the Lookout as a stop if you want a drink. The river: start at Charles/MGH, walk the Longfellow to Kendall, follow Memorial Drive west past MIT to the Harvard Bridge, cross back into Back Bay as the sun sets. Add the Weeks Footbridge if you continue to Harvard Square. The hills: the Orange Line connects Bunker Hill (Community College) at one end and Peters Hill (Forest Hills) at the other, with Copp's Hill a short walk from North Station in between.

On weather: Boston haze is worst on humid summer afternoons, when the skyline goes flat and grey from any distance. The clearest days are the cold ones, from October to April, after a front has come through. If you are buying a View Boston ticket, buy it for a morning after rain. If you are going to Spectacle Island, go on a day with a north-west wind.

On cost: every free viewpoint here is on the T for $2.40, and a 7-day pass covers the ferry too. The only things on this page that cost real money are View Boston and the Harbor Islands ferry, and neither is necessary for a great view. FAQ

Best views of Boston: common questions

Where is the best view of the Boston skyline?

For a free view, Piers Park in East Boston, one Blue Line stop from Downtown, has the most complete skyline view in the city, straight across the harbor. For a paid one, View Boston at the top of the Prudential Tower is the highest public viewpoint at 52 floors and looks over the whole city.

Is there a free observation deck in Boston?

Not a formal one. The Custom House Tower and the old Skywalk both charged, and View Boston at the Prudential charges admission. The free alternatives with height are the Bunker Hill Monument in Charlestown (294 steps, free), Copp's Hill, Dorchester Heights and Peters Hill at the Arnold Arboretum.

Where is the best place to watch the sunset in Boston?

The Charles River Esplanade and the Longfellow Bridge face west, so the sun sets behind Cambridge with the river in front of you. The Cambridge side along Memorial Drive gets the Back Bay skyline lit gold instead. Piers Park in East Boston gets the sun setting behind the Downtown towers.

How much does View Boston cost?

General admission is in the range of $30 to $35 for adults, with discounts for children and seniors and timed-entry tickets. Prices change seasonally and for sunset slots, so check the current price before you go.

What is the best view of Boston at night?

Fan Pier in the Seaport is the closest, brightest view of the lit Downtown skyline across Fort Point Channel. The Longfellow Bridge gives you the Back Bay towers reflected in the Charles. For height at night, View Boston stays open into the evening and the Lookout rooftop bar at the Envoy Hotel faces the skyline directly.

Can you see the Boston skyline from the water for cheap?

Yes. The MBTA ferry from Long Wharf to the Charlestown Navy Yard costs the same as a subway fare, takes about 10 minutes and gives you the harbor-side skyline both ways. The ferry to Spectacle Island in the Boston Harbor Islands runs seasonally for about $25 to $30 round trip and has the best long-range view of the city.
