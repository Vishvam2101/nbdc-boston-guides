A real plan, with real prices.

Boston is expensive for one thing: the bed. Almost everything else is cheap or free. This guide solves the bed, then shows you how not to pay for things that cost nothing. Prices are from September 2026.

The short answer

The 10 ways to do Boston cheap

  1. Sleep at HI Boston or in Cambridge
  2. Come November to March
  3. Buy the $22.50 7-day pass
  4. Freedom Trail, parks, Harborwalk: $0
  5. Time museums to free days
  6. North End at lunch, not dinner
  7. Chinatown, food halls, Haymarket
  8. Charlestown ferry, not a cruise
  9. Skyline from East Boston, free
  10. Skip airport taxis and duck boats

02

Where to stay in Boston cheaply

The biggest decision in a Boston budget, cheapest first.

HI Boston hostel

19 Stuart St, Theater DistrictBoylston or Chinatown, 3-minute walkDorms roughly $50–80

The city's main hostel, five minutes from Boston Common and the Freedom Trail. Dorms of 4 to 10 beds with lockers, breakfast included, a kitchen and free daily walking tours.

Local tip: dorms sell out weeks ahead in summer and around graduations.

Found Hotel Boston Common

79 Boylston St, Theater DistrictBoylston, 1-minute walkRooms roughly $110–200

A budget hotel facing the Common with small private rooms, some with shared bathrooms, plus a shared kitchen. The cheapest private room in central Boston most nights.

Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline

Red Line (Cambridge, Somerville) or Green Line (Brookline)Central, Porter, Davis; Coolidge CornerOften 20–40% below downtown

The best budget move is to not sleep in Boston. These are 10 to 20 minutes from Downtown with their own restaurants and cheaper rooms. Central, Porter and Davis Squares are the sweet spots; see where to stay.

Come in the off-season

Cheapest: January to early MarchNext: November and early DecemberPriciest: May, October, Marathon weekend

Rates can double between a Tuesday in February and a Saturday in October. Winter is cold but the museums and library are the point anyway. Details in the best time to visit Boston.

03

How to save on transit in Boston

  • Walk first. Downtown to the North End is 10 minutes, to the Seaport 15, to Copley 20.
  • Buy the 7-day pass for stays of three days or more; it covers subway, bus and inner-harbor ferries.
  • Buses 23, 28 and 29 are free through Roxbury, Dorchester and Mattapan.
  • The Charlestown ferry from Long Wharf is your harbor cruise, included in the pass.
  • Bluebikes for the Esplanade, Memorial Drive and the Southwest Corridor.
  • From the airport, take the free shuttle to Airport station and the Blue Line, or the free SL1 bus to South Station. More in getting around Boston.

04

Free attractions worth building days around

  • The Freedom Trail: 16 sites, 13 of them free to enter.
  • Boston Common, the Public Garden and the Esplanade at sunset.
  • The Harborwalk from the Seaport to the North End.
  • Bates Hall at the Boston Public Library, and Beacon Hill's streets.
  • Harvard Yard and the free Harvard Art Museums.
  • Piers Park in East Boston: a better skyline than any paid deck, for one Blue Line ride.

06

Eating in Boston for under $15

Eat your big meal at midday, in these neighborhoods.

The North End at lunch

Hanover and Salem StreetsHaymarket (Orange/Green)Weekday lunch$5–15

Galleria Umberto, 289 Hanover, sells Sicilian pizza squares and arancini for a few dollars, cash only, until it runs out. Cannoli at Mike's or Modern are under $6.

Chinatown

Around Beach St and Harrison AveChinatown (Orange)Lunch and late night$8–15

The cheapest sit-down dinner in central Boston: dumplings, noodles, dim sum, pho. Several places stay open past midnight, and it is next door to HI Boston.

Food halls and markets

Boston Public Market, 100 Hanover St; Time Out Market, 401 Park Dr; Quincy MarketHaymarket; Fenway; Government CenterDaily$8–18

Boston Public Market is good for a $10 lunch; Time Out Market has counter versions of well-known restaurants. Fridays and Saturdays, Haymarket on Blackstone Street sells the cheapest produce in the city.

Student neighborhoods

Allston, Central Square, Davis Square, Mission HillHarvard Ave; Central, Davis; Brigham CircleAny time$8–15

Priced for 150,000 students. Allston has Korean, Vietnamese and Brazilian food on Harvard Avenue; Central and Davis Squares are the same idea. The cheap bars are here too.

09

Money-saving mistakes to avoid in Boston

  • Taking a taxi from the airport: $30–45 versus $2.40 on the Blue Line.
  • Renting a car: hotel parking is $40–60 a night and everything is on the T.
  • Paying full museum price on the wrong day: check the table above and move your museum day.
  • North End at dinner: reverse it. Lunch there, dinner in Chinatown or Allston.
  • Paying for a harbor cruise or observation deck: the Charlestown ferry and Piers Park do the same job; see best views of Boston.
  • Skipping the library: free, beautiful, with clean bathrooms and wifi in the middle of Back Bay.

FAQ

Boston on a budget: common questions

How much does a trip to Boston cost per day?

About $90 to $120 a day in a hostel dorm eating cheaply. Mid-budget, in a modest hotel or Cambridge room, is $200 to $280. The bed is the expensive part.

Is Boston expensive to visit?

Hotels are, and restaurants are pricey at dinner. Almost everything else is cheap or free: the Freedom Trail, parks, Harborwalk, library, museum free days and a $22.50 7-day T pass.

What is the cheapest way to get around Boston?

Walking. Central neighborhoods are 30 to 40 minutes apart on foot. Otherwise the $22.50 7-day pass covers subway, bus and the Charlestown ferry, and buses 23, 28 and 29 are free.

Where can I stay cheaply in Boston?

HI Boston hostel at 19 Stuart Street, Found Hotel at 79 Boylston Street, or Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline on the Red or Green Lines. Come November to March for the lowest rates.

Which Boston museums are free?

Harvard Art Museums every day. ICA Thursday evenings. MFA pay-what-you-wish Wednesdays after 4pm. MIT Museum the last Sunday of the month. USS Constitution Museum by donation.
